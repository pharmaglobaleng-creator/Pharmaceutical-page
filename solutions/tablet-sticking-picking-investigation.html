What should you investigate first?

Start with where material accumulates, which tools are affected and when the defect appears. Document the punch face and corresponding tablet, then compare tooling, formulation and process records. Sticking and picking can have several contributors; the visible defect alone does not establish the root cause.

Keep the technical and financial decisions connected. A treatment is only useful if it meets tooling and product requirements. A favorable cost scenario does not establish that a coating, polishing operation or process change is suitable.

Show the formulas and assumptions

Monthly stop hours = batches × stops per batch × minutes per stop ÷ 60. Avoidable stop labor = hours × people × loaded hourly cost × avoidable labor %. Stop-linked cost pool = avoidable stop labor + cleaning supplies + avoidable machine costs.

Other cost pool = investigation + rework + polishing + (replacement cost × annual replacement events ÷ 12). Monthly savings = stop-linked pool × stop reduction + reject cost × reject reduction + other pool × other reduction. Monthly net cash benefit = savings − added recurring cost.

Annual net benefit = monthly net × 12. First-year net subtracts the one-time investment. Simple payback = investment ÷ positive monthly net benefit. Twelve-month break-even stop reduction = (investment ÷ selected target months + recurring cost − reject savings − other savings) ÷ stop-linked cost pool. A negative threshold is shown as 0%; a threshold above 100% is not achievable from stops alone under these assumptions.

Capacity value = recovered stop hours × user-entered incremental contribution per hour, shown separately. No tax, financing, discounting or delayed ramp-up is modeled. Include implementation downtime and qualification costs in the one-time cost if applicable. Constant volume, stop duration and proportional cost reductions are assumed.

A fully worked fictional example

In USD, 20 batches × 2 stops × 30 minutes gives 20 stopped hours a month. Two people at $40/hour allocate $1,600 of labor; at 50% avoidability, $800 enters the cash model. Cleaning adds $200, making the stop-linked pool $1,000/month. Rejects cost $400/month. Investigation, rework, polishing and replacement add $350/month.

At 50% fewer stops, 25% fewer rejects and 20% lower other costs, gross savings are $500 + $100 + $70 = $670/month. Subtract $100 recurring cost: $570/month or $6,840/year. A $4,000 investment leaves $2,840 first-year net benefit and a 7.0-month simple payback. Twelve-month break-even requires 26.33% fewer stops, holding the other assumptions constant. The low/high scenarios yield $3,120/$10,560 annual net benefit. These are illustrative arithmetic inputs, not customer outcomes.

Make a trial result worth trusting

1. Establish the baseline

Record a defined observation period, product/lot reference, tooling IDs, stops, cleaning time and rejected quantity. Preserve photos and the relevant quality results.

2. Define the change

Specify the proposed inspection, polishing, restoration or coating evaluation. Agree acceptance criteria, costs and the controlled trial plan with responsible staff before implementation.

3. Record the outcome

Compare like-for-like exposure and document other changes. Record actual costs and whether product and tooling requirements were met. Replace assumptions in the model with measured results.

Use the existing defect investigation wizard for conditional hypotheses and the tooling inspection checklist for inspection planning.

What should an investigation handover contain?

1. A traceable observation

Record the press, tooling set, upper/lower tool and station IDs. Describe where the defect occurs and when it starts. Preserve the observation period and batch reference.

2. Comparable photographs

Include a station context view, punch-face view, engraving detail and the corresponding tablet. Keep original files, orientation and a suitable scale reference. Record before/after cleaning status.

3. A controlled comparison

State the proposed change, acceptance criteria and observation window. Record other changes and compare rates per batch or tablet, not unmatched totals.

Questions to settle before the trial

  • Which observation would support or contradict the proposed explanation?
  • Who approves the change, tooling inspection and product-quality criteria?
  • What findings require stopping, segregating tooling or escalating the review?
  • Which costs can actually be avoided, and which represent recovered capacity?
  • What records will demonstrate the outcome and document any unintended effects?

Use your site’s approved procedures for equipment isolation, inspection, sampling and change control. ICH Q10 section 3.2.3 describes evaluation before implementation and evaluation after a change.

Example: fewer defects, or just a shorter run?

Rate-comparison example (separate from the financial example): a baseline has 20 stops in 10 batches. A trial has 20 stops in 20 batches. The total number of stops is unchanged, but stops per batch fall from 2 to 1: a 50% reduction in that rate.

That comparison still needs comparable products, batch sizes and observation methods. Also check stop duration, rejects and quality results. A lower stop rate alone does not prove that a tooling change caused the improvement.

Tablet sticking and picking investigation: 15 questions answered

Practical facts about evidence collection, tooling assessment and the cost model. Technical background is linked in Sources and methodology.

What is the difference between tablet sticking and picking?

Sticking involves material adhering to a punch face. Picking commonly describes localized adhesion associated with lettering or other engraved details. Record the location and appearance of the defect before choosing an investigation route.

Does powder inside punch numbers mean I need a new coating?

No. Engraving geometry, surface condition, formulation and process conditions can all warrant review. A coating candidate needs evaluation against the actual product and tooling requirements.

Can polishing restore every worn punch?

No. The decision depends on inspection, dimensions, tip geometry and the nature of any damage. An acceptable finish does not by itself establish that a tool is suitable for production.

Can this calculator prove a tooling treatment will work?

No. It calculates the consequences of assumptions you enter. A controlled trial must establish whether performance improves while the required product-quality attributes remain acceptable.

Are recovered production hours the same as cash savings?

No. Recovered capacity may have value, but it is not cash saved automatically. Labor allocations may also remain payable. Confirm which costs are genuinely avoidable before making an investment decision.

What should I send for an initial assessment?

Provide the press and tooling reference, a description of the defect, affected tool IDs, clear photographs, recurrence timing and relevant inspection history. Share sensitive product information only through your approved company process.

How should I compare a trial with the baseline?

Define the observation period and measures before the trial. Record comparable batches, stop duration, rejected quantity and quality results; document other changes that could affect the comparison.

What should a tablet sticking and picking investigation record first?

Record the product and batch reference, press, tooling set, affected station IDs, defect location and when it first appears. Pair tablet photographs with punch-face observations. This creates a traceable starting point for comparing affected and acceptable runs; it does not establish the cause by itself.

Can a tablet photograph identify the root cause?

A photograph documents appearance, not causation. Similar visible defects can require different investigations. Preserve the original image and connect it to tool identity, process records and inspection findings. The illustrative images in this guide are teaching aids, not measured customer results or acceptance standards.

Why record recurrence after punch cleaning?

Recurrence records distinguish a temporary improvement after cleaning from a sustained change. Record the approved cleaning method and the elapsed time, tablets or batches until the defect returns. Compare like-for-like runs rather than treating a clean-looking punch as evidence that the underlying problem has been resolved.

Why compare defect rates instead of total counts?

Counts depend on how much production was observed. Twenty stops in ten comparable batches is two stops per batch; twenty stops in twenty batches is one. Choose a denominator appropriate to the comparison, and also record batch size, stop duration and rejects so a lower rate is not mistaken for a complete improvement.

What does the break-even stop reduction mean?

It is the modeled reduction in stop-linked avoidable cash costs needed to recover the one-time investment within the selected 6-, 12- or 24-month horizon. Mid-case reject savings and other savings remain fixed. The threshold is a financial target based on your inputs, not a prediction of treatment effectiveness.

Why does changing currency leave the calculated numbers unchanged?

The currency selector changes the currency label and formatting; it does not apply exchange rates. Enter every cost in the same currency before comparing cases. Mixing currencies in one scenario produces a misleading financial result even if the arithmetic is internally consistent.

Which records belong in a tooling assessment handover?

Include the problem description, tool and station map, dated photographs, baseline production measures, inspection history and proposed trial criteria. Separate observed facts from assumptions. Share only information approved for disclosure and use the workbook to retain baseline, planned-trial and measured-outcome records.

Turn the evidence into an assessment request

Send the press model, tooling reference, affected station IDs, clear punch-face and tablet photos, and recurrence timing. Include your completed worksheet if appropriate. Ask for a written assessment scope and quotation, including any additional information needed and a proposed schedule. Response and service timing will be confirmed individually.
