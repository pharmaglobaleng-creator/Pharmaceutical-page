Tablet Chipping: Find Where Edge Damage Begins

Browse 25 quick answers

Tablet chipping is localized loss of material from an edge. The first useful question is where intact tablets become damaged: at ejection, take-off, transfer, coating or packaging. That location guides the investigation and helps distinguish contact damage from a tablet that cannot withstand the handling it receives.

Request an Evaluation

Describe the missing material before assigning a cause

Record whether damage affects the upper rim, lower rim, both edges, a corner or an area beside a score. Include tablet orientation and a scale in photographs where your sample-handling procedure permits. Keep intact comparison tablets from the same period. A picture of loose fragments alone may not establish which surface failed.

Distinguish a small edge chip from capping, where a crown separates, and lamination, where the body splits into layers. A tablet may show more than one defect. Count each pattern separately so a trial that reduces large chips does not conceal a rise in crown separation. Use one agreed defect description across production, maintenance and quality records.

Find the earliest confirmed damaged sample

Map the product route from the earliest safe post-ejection sampling point through take-off, discharge, dedusting, transfer and later operations. Compare samples before and after the first suspected contact. Do not bypass guards or reach into moving equipment to obtain a closer view; use authorized observation and sampling methods.

If samples are intact before a transfer and damaged afterward, inspect that transition before replacing the punch set. If the earliest sample is already chipped, the investigation remains upstream. State where evidence is missing: damage detected after coating could have started earlier and become visible later. Preserve the sample times, equipment status and run stage so the comparison can be repeated after a change.

Use the affected edge to focus the take-off review

Natoli’s ejection guidance identifies different inspection areas for upper-edge and lower-edge chipping. It associates upper-edge damage with take-off-bar positioning and lower-edge damage with the relationship between the lower punch, die table and discharge path.[1] These are useful starting points for an equipment-specific inspection, not proof that a particular adjustment is required.

Have the responsible technician compare the installed take-off components and tablet orientation with the press instructions. Record the setup as found and any interference marks. For a defect that repeats at particular stations, retain the station identities and corresponding lower punches. Ask whether their inspection results explain the pattern. Use tablet press troubleshooting to organize the mechanical review, including the information needed from the equipment manufacturer.

Assess strength without making hardness the only target

A tablet can satisfy one mechanical measurement and still be vulnerable to a particular handling event. Breaking force, friability and the observed edge damage describe different aspects of performance. Compare the applicable test results with the product requirements and with an acceptable run, using the same sample age, geometry and method.

A formulation study examining friability evaluated multiple material and tablet mechanical properties together rather than treating a single hardness reading as a complete prediction.[2] If strength is a credible part of the problem, investigate it through the hardness optimization guide. Increasing compression force merely to stop chips requires review of the other product attributes and tooling limits. A tougher-looking edge is not sufficient verification by itself.

Inspect the tablet edge geometry and the matching tool

Compare the tablet drawing with the punch cup, land, score and corner details. Record whether the defect follows one feature or appears randomly around the perimeter. Bring the affected upper punch, lower punch and die information into the same review. Document deposits before cleaning where permitted, then inspect the underlying surfaces separately.

Research comparing modified punch-face edges found different tablet physical quality under the studied conditions.[3] That supports a controlled design review when the geometry is implicated. It does not authorize rounding a land, widening a score or changing the tablet shape during routine polishing. Any proposed reconditioning must preserve specified dimensions or follow an approved design change. See tablet punch restoration for the inspection information to supply.

Include coating and transfer when tablets leave the press intact

A study of film-coating breakage evaluated tablet properties, equipment design and process conditions together.[4] The practical implication is to investigate the operation where damage develops, rather than assigning every broken tablet to compression. Compare incoming cores with samples taken at an appropriate stage of the coating operation.

For transfers and packaging, document the route, contact points and the timing of damage. Include changes in containers, chutes or product orientation if they coincide with the issue. Where a product or equipment change requires specialist assessment, identify that need in the work plan. Avoid trialing several unrelated handling and compression changes simultaneously unless the combined test is deliberately defined.

Use a consistent edge map instead of “damaged tablet”

Choose a repeatable way to describe locations on the tablet, such as the upper face, lower face, band, score end and individual corners of a shaped tablet. Keep the orientation reference in each image. A chip beside an identifier and a chip on the opposite rim may require different comparisons even when their size looks similar. Record the location before selecting a defect category.

Photograph the intact surrounding edge as well as the missing portion. Where permitted, include a retained fragment without attempting to force it back into place. Note whether damage appears as one localized break, several separate losses or more general edge erosion. These are observations for the investigation, not a way to diagnose material strength or tool condition from the photograph alone.

Use the same map when comparing tools and downstream contact points. If a feature repeatedly breaks on one side, the reviewer needs to know how that side was oriented during take-off and later transfer. Where orientation cannot be recovered, record the gap. Do not assign an upper- or lower-punch cause merely because a chipped tablet was photographed with one face pointing upward.

Build a comparison across a single handling boundary

To investigate one transfer, identify the last accessible sampling point upstream and the first useful point downstream. Agree how samples will be collected and handled so the sampling process does not introduce a new, uncontrolled impact. Record the time window and operating state. Samples collected hours apart may describe different production conditions rather than the effect of the transfer between them.

Keep the original defect categories and denominators on both sides. If a few upstream tablets already have small chips, the downstream comparison needs to distinguish new damage from enlargement of existing damage where the available evidence allows it. A higher downstream count can justify closer examination of that stage, but it does not identify the exact contact or establish that all incoming tablets were sound.

If the route contains several transitions, review them in a sequence that can narrow the interval where damage first becomes detectable. Do not modify every chute at once simply because they all contact the product. Preserve the configuration as found, the changes actually made and the resulting observations. A well-defined stage comparison supports a focused equipment discussion and avoids attributing later handling damage to the punch set without evidence.

Review shape changes as product decisions

For a shaped or scored tablet, compare the recurring chip with the approved drawing and intended product appearance. A corner, score termination or narrow region may deserve a specific geometry review when the damage consistently follows it. Give the design reviewer photographs of acceptable and affected tablets, the corresponding tooling information and the point where the damage is observed.

Keep the purpose of the feature visible in the discussion. A score, logo or identifier may serve a requirement that cannot be removed simply to obtain a more robust shape. Ask what alternative would preserve the required function and what product checks would be needed. This guide does not authorize changing a scored tablet’s division characteristics or substituting a different approved appearance.

If the proposed action is restoration rather than redesign, make the boundary explicit. The work must be assessed against the existing specification and available material, with the relevant edge and cup measurements retained. A temporary reduction in visible chips after extensive rounding does not demonstrate an acceptable repair if the tool no longer reproduces the required tablet geometry. Evaluate both the defect response and the resulting tool condition.

Example: similar chips, different evidence

Consider two illustrative reports. In the first, a new lower-edge chip appears immediately after a discharge component is replaced, while upstream samples remain acceptable. In the second, edge damage appears at several locations and becomes evident through multiple handling stages, without a documented equipment change. Both are called chipping, but they do not justify starting with the same replacement or press adjustment.

The first report calls for a close comparison of the changed component, installation and actual transition where damage appears. The second needs matched mechanical-test results, sample history and an assessment of the handling demands. These are investigation priorities, not diagnoses. The first product may also have a strength limitation, and the second may still encounter an unsuitable contact; preserve evidence that challenges the initial explanation.

For each case, write a different test question before acting. One might ask whether correcting an identified installation discrepancy changes lower-edge damage. The other might ask which stage first separates acceptable incoming samples from affected ones. Keep the required tablet properties in both plans. The value of the comparison is that it directs an answerable next step rather than offering one universal cure for every missing edge.

Verify a remedy using the same damage definition

Agree the sampling locations, sample quantities and observation period before the trial. Record both the number examined and the number showing the specified chip pattern; counts without a denominator are hard to compare. Keep the upper-edge, lower-edge and other fracture categories separate when their mechanisms may differ.

Compare baseline and trial under equivalent conditions where possible. If output, tablet mass or downstream handling changed, disclose the difference. Check whether the original defect returns later in the run and whether another failure mode appears. A passing short sample supports only the conditions actually tested. Include the applicable product tests and the responsible review before closing the investigation.

Prepare a focused request for tooling support

Send PharmaGlobalEng clear face and side photographs, the first stage where chipping is confirmed, the tablet shape and the press and tooling identities. Add the pattern across stations, inspection findings and the outcome of previous changes. If the tablet is sound upstream, include the downstream equipment involved rather than sending only punch photographs.

Request an assessment of the observed condition and the measurements needed for a decision. The tooling evaluation page helps organize that request. Confirm whether the proposed scope is inspection, restoration assessment or a referral for design or equipment review. The objective is to explain the damage and verify an appropriate change, not to purchase a coating before establishing why material is being lost.

25 practical questions and answers

Quick answers to the questions covered in this guide. Use the detailed sections above for the evidence and context behind each answer.

01

What is tablet chipping?

Tablet chipping is localized loss of material from an edge. Identify the affected location and the first stage where damage is confirmed before selecting a tooling or equipment remedy.

02

Is edge chipping the same as picking?

No. Picking concerns localized loss associated with retained material at punch detail. An edge chip needs its own location and timing investigation rather than an automatic picking label.

03

How is chipping different from capping?

Chipping removes a localized edge piece; capping separates a crown from the tablet body. Record the actual pattern and keep both categories visible if they occur together.

04

Which edge locations should be recorded?

Distinguish upper rim, lower rim, band, corners and score-related locations. Use a consistent orientation reference so the same feature can be compared across tablets, tools and process stages.

05

What photographs are useful for edge damage?

Show the missing portion and the surrounding intact edge, with orientation and an appropriate scale. Include acceptable comparison tablets and preserve the sampling point and time.

06

Can a photograph alone identify why an edge broke?

No. It documents the visible damage but cannot establish the contact, material response or tool condition responsible. Pair images with the process route and corresponding inspection evidence.

07

Why compare samples before and after a transfer?

The comparison helps narrow where damage first becomes detectable. Use comparable operating periods and approved sampling methods so the collection process does not introduce a different impact.

08

Does finding chips after coating prove coating caused them?

No. Preserve upstream samples and handling history. Damage may have existed earlier or become visible later, so the discovery point is not automatically the point of origin.

09

What can upper-edge damage prompt an inspector to review?

Manufacturer guidance identifies take-off positioning as a relevant inspection area. Compare the installed arrangement with the press instructions; the location alone does not establish a required adjustment.

10

What can lower-edge damage prompt an inspector to review?

Review the relevant lower-punch, die-table and discharge relationships using equipment instructions. Record the actual setup and contact evidence before concluding that any one component caused the chip.

11

Should every chip trigger higher compression force?

No. Compare the damage stage and applicable mechanical tests first. A force change must be evaluated against other product attributes and tooling limits, not appearance alone.

12

Does a passing hardness result guarantee resistance to chipping?

No. Breaking force, friability and observed handling damage provide different evidence. Assess the applicable product requirements and the operation where the edge damage develops.

13

Why inspect the matching punch and die?

Relate the tablet location to the actual cup, land, score and die information. Keep deposits, permanent damage and measured geometry distinct before deciding whether restoration is justified.

14

Can a tablet corner be rounded during routine polishing to stop chips?

A geometry change needs the appropriate design review. Restoration should preserve specified features; a better-looking trial is not an acceptable repair if the required tablet shape has changed.

15

Why keep a score or identifier’s function in the review?

The feature may serve a product requirement that cannot simply be removed. Any alternative should preserve its required function and include the appropriate product and tooling checks.

16

What should a downstream chipping record include?

Retain the product route, contact points, equipment configuration, timing and matched samples. Include relevant changes in chutes, containers or orientation when they coincide with the observed damage.

17

How can a station pattern help?

A confirmed pattern directs comparison toward corresponding tools and equipment records. If tablet-to-station identity is unavailable, state that limitation rather than assigning a cause from appearance.

18

What if incoming tablets already have small chips?

Keep that baseline visible when assessing downstream damage. Distinguish new damage from possible enlargement where evidence permits, instead of assuming every downstream defect began at the transfer.

19

Why avoid modifying several transfers simultaneously?

A combined change may improve output without identifying which contact mattered. Preserve each configuration and use a defined comparison when the investigation needs to isolate a particular stage.

20

What should a chipping trial measure?

Record the defined chip categories, number examined, sampling stages and relevant product tests. Compare equivalent conditions and include the period when the original damage tended to appear.

21

Can fewer chips in a smaller sample establish improvement?

Not without a valid comparison. Keep the denominator and sampling method beside each count so a reduced observation effort is not mistaken for a lower defect rate.

22

How should different chipping patterns be reported?

Retain upper-edge, lower-edge, corner and other relevant categories separately. Improvement in one pattern should not conceal another persistent defect or a new fracture mode.

23

What does a brief successful startup establish?

It supports only the conditions and period sampled. If damage historically developed later or downstream, the verification must address those stages before the investigation is considered resolved.

24

What should a restoration request explain?

State the observed condition, implicated feature, available measurements and protected geometry. Ask whether recovery is technically suitable before selecting polishing, coating or replacement as the predetermined answer.

25

What information belongs in a chipping inquiry?

Send face and side photographs, first confirmed damage stage, tablet shape, press and tooling identities, station information and previous trial outcomes. Include downstream equipment details where relevant.
