What tablet picking looks like

Picking is localized loss of tablet material where product adheres to a detail on the punch, often lettering or a logo. Broad material buildup across the punch face is a different observation. A chipped edge alone is not proof of picking.[1]

Before cleaning, photograph the affected tablets and the corresponding punch face where your procedures allow it. Identify the upper or lower tool and station. Put an unaffected tablet beside the damaged example, keeping the same orientation and lighting. That makes it easier to see whether the defect follows a particular feature or simply appears in different places.

Do not write only “bad logo” in the record. A more useful description is “material missing inside the left side of the letter on the upper face; first observed after the last documented acceptable sample.” Add the actual station and sample time. This is a suggested recording format, not a diagnosis.

Use the pattern to narrow the inspection

A repeating mark gives you a place to investigate. It does not, by itself, identify the cause. The comparisons below are intended to help organize an inspection rather than prescribe a fix.

Keep the comparison traceable. Photographing an unidentified punch from a tray makes it difficult to connect an observation to the tablet that came from it. When station-level sampling is not available, record that limitation instead of guessing.

Inspect the engraving without changing it

Tablet design can affect picking and other manufacturing problems.[2] For an engraving review, request the approved drawing and inspect the feature that corresponds to the defect. Note retained product, uneven edges, corrosion, scratches and differences from the reference tool. Include the existing coating identity rather than identifying it by color alone.

Keep two separate records: the condition before cleaning, which documents the deposit, and the condition after approved cleaning, which helps reveal the underlying surface. Use comparable photographs so residue is not mistaken for permanent damage.

Do not deepen a letter, open a narrow detail or round an edge as an informal trial. First establish whether the feature still matches its approved geometry and whether any proposed work is permitted. A change that improves appearance but alters tablet identification is not an acceptable shortcut.

Useful questions for a tooling review include: Is the damage confined to one detail? Is there enough dimensional allowance for the proposed work? Has the same tool been polished repeatedly? Can the original feature be preserved? An answer of “not yet measured” is more useful than an unsupported assurance.

Choose between cleaning, polishing, coating and replacement

Cleaning assessment: establish whether the visible obstruction is removable product and whether the approved cleaning method reaches the affected detail. Avoid confusing cleanability with a permanent change in release behavior.

Controlled polishing: consider an evaluation when recoverable surface damage is present. Tool maintenance guidance calls for measurement after repair; removing a mark is not enough when critical dimensions also have to remain acceptable.[4] Agree which surfaces can be worked and which features must remain unchanged.

Coating evaluation: discuss a candidate treatment when the substrate and application justify it. Ask what the treatment is intended to address, how compatibility will be assessed, and what evidence will be collected in a trial. Our punch-coatings page explains the questions to resolve before selecting a surface treatment.

Replacement or engineering review: a crack, unacceptable dimensional loss or a feature that cannot meet its requirements is not a cosmetic polishing problem. Do not approve unsuitable tooling simply because replacement has a long lead time.

Before ordering new punches: send PharmaGlobalEng the defect photographs and available inspection results. Where the tooling is suitable, a polishing or surface-engineering route may avoid unnecessary replacement. We need to assess the tools before confirming scope or turnaround.

Review lubrication without guessing a concentration

A study of two model drugs linked magnesium-stearate surface coverage to punch-sticking propensity and examined concentration, drug loading, particle size and mixing conditions.[3] That supports looking beyond the amount on the batch sheet; it does not supply a universal percentage for your product.

For the investigation, request the lubricant grade, addition sequence, recorded quantity, mixing time and any process changes. Compare these with a successful run. Keep formulation decisions with the responsible development and quality teams, and record any approved changes separately from tooling work.

Judge the result over a meaningful run

Write the acceptance question before the trial. For example: does the same engraving remain legible over the agreed observation period without increasing cleaning interruptions or other defects? Record the baseline and use the same sampling approach for the comparison.

Track the defect location, affected samples, elapsed run time, cleaning stops and relevant operating conditions. Review tablet appearance together with the product’s required quality tests. Avoid declaring success from a photograph alone, or from fewer rejects collected over a much shorter run.

Where several factors have to change together, document the plan and its limitations. A better result may justify further work without proving which individual change produced it. Retain that distinction in the final investigation.

Questions about recurring picking

Should every picking problem receive a new coating?

No automatic treatment should be selected from the defect name. First establish the condition of the engraving, the existing surface and the relevant production history. A proposed coating needs an application-specific reason and a way to check performance.

Are five good tablets after cleaning enough to close the investigation?

They document that sample, not the rest of the run. Use the sampling plan and observation period agreed by your manufacturing and quality teams, including the period when the original problem normally appeared.

What should I send for a preliminary review?

Send both tablet faces, the corresponding punch detail, the press model, tooling identification, coating records and the time the defect appears. Include what has already been tried and whether it changed the pattern.
