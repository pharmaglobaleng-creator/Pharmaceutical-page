What is the difference between tablet sticking and picking?

Sticking is formulation adhering to the punch face. Picking is localized loss from the tablet where material is retained around punch detail, such as lettering or a logo. Record where the material remains and where it is missing; the names alone do not identify the cause.

Tablet sticking vs. picking: symptoms and tooling services

Use the location of the deposit and the corresponding tablet defect to describe the problem. Both can occur in the same run; the appropriate correction depends on the tooling condition and formulation.

Start with the tablet and its corresponding punch. Photograph both faces, preserve upper/lower orientation and record the tooling set, station where known, batch and sampling time. The patterns below select the next investigation; none proves a cause by itself.

A practical decision sequence

  1. Locate: broad face deposit, local engraved detail or die-wall mark?
  2. Map: one tool, a repeated feature across tools or the whole set?
  3. Compare: what changed between acceptable and affected production?
  4. Evaluate: define one correction or a designed study with explicit acceptance requirements.
  5. Verify: compare the same defect measures over a relevant run duration.

For closer inspection of a known pattern, use the existing broad sticking guide or picking around numbers, letters and logos guide. Keep this page as the combined investigation starting point.

Separate formulation causes from tooling and process causes

Adhesion to a punch and bonding within a tablet interact. Research across multiple compounds describes different accumulation patterns depending on interactions among the API, excipients and punch surface. A deposit therefore does not establish that a punch is defective. [1]

Moisture and the production environment

Review the moisture test method, sampling point, storage and hold times, and recorded room conditions. Research on moisture during compression links material moisture, heat and processing conditions; its results do not supply a universal moisture or humidity limit for every formulation. Compare like-for-like measurements and have the formulation team define any drying or conditioning study. [5]

Lubricant addition and mixing

Check lubricant identity, amount, addition sequence and mixing history against an acceptable batch. Lubrication can reduce adhesion while also reducing tablet tensile strength. More lubricant is not automatically a better correction. Keep required product-quality tests in the study rather than judging success only by a cleaner punch. [1]

Particle properties and powder behavior

Review ingredient grade, particle-size information and granulation changes. A study of 24 compounds identified particle surface area and tablet tensile strength as important factors in its sticking dataset. Flow behavior alone cannot establish punch-release performance. Use available material-characterization results to choose a comparison, not to assign a cause from a single powder measurement. [6]

Investigation prompt: if several previously acceptable tools develop the same defect after a batch change, review the material records alongside tooling inspection. If a defect is confined to one damaged feature, prioritize that feature without excluding a formulation interaction.

Inspect punch faces, lettering and engraving

Surface damage versus deposits

Document the punch before cleaning where procedures permit, then inspect the cleaned surface. Compare suspect marks with acceptable tools and the approved geometry. A deposit can conceal a scratch or look like damage. Request tooling inspection before specifying repeated polishing.

Where damage is recoverable, define the surface to restore and the dimensions, cup profile, tip land and engraving to preserve. Punch restoration cannot replace material already lost beyond the permitted geometry.

Why logos and characters need a drawing review

Enclosed character areas, internal corners and identifier placement can contribute to picking. Natoli's authors describe pre-pick and tapered engraving as design options, rather than corrections to improvise during polishing. Ask the supplier to review the affected feature and explain how a revision preserves the required tablet identity. [2]

Use paired tablet/punch images with the affected feature marked. Record whether it repeats across all matching engravings or only on selected tools. A common feature suggests a different comparison from a single damaged tool.

Does a mirror finish always release better?

No. Natoli reports that some products respond better to a matte surface. Finish should be evaluated with the actual formulation rather than selected by visual shine alone. [2] Agree the intended finish and dimensional checks with the polishing service before work begins.

Review compression force, dwell time and press speed together

Compression force is a study variable, not a universal fix

Higher force may help some formulations form a stronger compact, but published experiments with five APIs found greater sticking at higher compaction pressures. The direction of the response depends on the material and conditions. [7] Do not prescribe a force increase from the defect name alone.

Record precompression and main-compression settings separately, with actual force information where available, press identity, punch geometry and tablet dimensions. Force and pressure are not interchangeable when comparing tools with different projected areas. See the existing compression force investigation for the broader process review.

Dwell time and speed

Dwell time describes time under compression and depends on press/tooling geometry as well as speed. An extended head flat can increase dwell at a given speed; it requires a compatible tooling design. [8] Record the press and head configuration so an RPM value is not treated as an equivalent compression history on every machine. Use the dwell time guide for that separate setup question.

For a speed comparison, document the other settings and sample at agreed intervals. Include startup and the period when the defect previously emerged. Review any change in product quality alongside deposit formation, throughput and interruptions.

Choose polishing, restoration or a coating for the supported cause

Polishing addresses a surface condition; an engraving revision addresses geometry; a coating changes surface properties. The proposed work should identify which observation it addresses. Applying all three at once may improve production but will not isolate the contribution of each change.

How to evaluate punch coatings

CrN, DLC and TiN are among the coating families discussed in Natoli's technical guidance. Their names alone do not predict performance for a product. Coating thickness, adhesion, surface roughness, wear and corrosion behavior all matter in selection. [4]

Ask the supplier to document compatibility with the tool material, existing treatment, required dimensions, cleaning method and intended formulation. Compare product release over a representative run, then inspect the treated surface. Do not infer an anti-sticking guarantee from coating hardness or another customer's batch count. Use the coating evaluation service to discuss the candidate treatment and trial scope.

Verify the correction and retain a usable investigation record

Sticking can accumulate over repeated compactions, so a short successful restart may miss the original failure pattern. Thomas and colleagues studied repeated compaction as a way to detect sticking propensity; that research supports observing progression, not assuming a single tablet establishes production performance. [3]

Record before and after the change

  • Product/batch, press, tooling set, upper/lower face and station identity where available.
  • Paired photographs, acceptable and affected samples, collection times and sampling interval.
  • Moisture and lubricant records, compression settings, speed and relevant environmental observations.
  • Tooling dimensions, surface condition, coating identity, cleaning history and previous treatment.
  • Change evaluated, run duration, first observed deposit, localized picking, rejected units and cleaning interruptions.
  • Required product-quality results, acceptance criteria and any unresolved or conflicting evidence.

Use consistent sampling and denominators when comparing reject rates. Keep broad filming and localized picking as separate observations. The responsible production, formulation and quality teams should define permitted changes and acceptance requirements; this guide supplies no universal operating setpoints.

Illustrative example: the face improves but one letter still picks

In a hypothetical trial, broad deposits decrease after surface treatment but the same letter continues to lose detail. Record both outcomes. Inspect the remaining local feature against the drawing instead of calling the entire defect solved. If the trial ended before the original buildup appeared, extend the evaluation as agreed before drawing a production conclusion. 25 practical questions and answers

Quick answers to the questions covered in this guide. Use the detailed sections above for the evidence and context behind each answer.

01

What distinction starts a sticking-versus-picking investigation?

Locate where material remains and where it is missing. Broad punch-face deposits indicate a different observation from localized loss around lettering or a logo, even when the two defects appear together.

02

Can sticking and picking occur on the same tablet?

Yes. Record broad deposits and localized loss separately so an improvement in one category does not conceal a remaining problem in the other during treatment selection or trial evaluation.

03

Why pair tablet photographs with the corresponding punches?

The pairing connects the visible defect with the actual tool feature and identity. Without it, a damaged tablet alone cannot establish whether the same local condition repeats at one station.

04

What can cause a broad film across several punch faces?

Formulation, process conditions and tool surfaces can all contribute. Compare batch and moisture records, lubricant history, compression settings and inspected surfaces before selecting a controlled correction.

05

Why does picking recur around the same letter or logo?

A repeated local defect warrants comparison of the corresponding engraving, retained material and approved drawing. Check whether it affects every matching feature or only selected tools before deciding between design review and surface recovery.

06

What if the main problem is difficult ejection or band marking?

Begin with the die-wall binding investigation and retain the sampling stage. Do not automatically classify a sidewall mark or release problem as punch-face sticking without the corresponding evidence.

07

How is die-wall binding different from punch-face sticking?

Binding concerns release at the die wall and may involve band marks or difficult ejection. Punch-face sticking concerns deposits on the punch. Preserve the location and sampling stage rather than treating both as the same defect.

08

Which records preserve the timing of a sticking or picking defect?

Record the first observed defect, sampling interval, run duration and related operating conditions. Distinguish continuous observation from occasional samples so that the reported onset does not imply more precision than the evidence supports.

09

Should we increase lubricant to stop sticking?

Not automatically. Lubrication can reduce adhesion but can also reduce tablet tensile strength. Review addition and mixing records, and have the formulation team evaluate changes with the required product-quality tests.

10

What should be compared between affected and unaffected punches?

Compare surface condition, dimensions, cleaning history, coating identity and prior work within the same campaign where practical. A localized difference guides the next inspection without excluding other contributors automatically.

11

How should localized picking around engraving be examined?

Identify the exact feature and compare it with the approved drawing after appropriate cleaning. Record whether the same location collects material across several tools, rather than treating every local defect as identical.

12

Can pre-pick or tapered engraving help with picking?

They are design options for selected character features. Have the tooling supplier review the affected geometry and preserve required tablet identity; do not improvise an engraving change during polishing.

13

What should be checked before restoring a picking-related surface?

Establish that inspection supports recoverable damage and that sufficient allowance remains to retain the required geometry. Repeated polishing cannot recreate an engraving or profile already outside its requirements.

14

Does a mirror-polished punch always release better?

No. Some products respond better to a different finish. Evaluate the proposed surface with the actual formulation and dimensional requirements instead of selecting it by visual shine alone.

15

Which formulation and process records belong in the comparison?

Retain lubricant addition and mixing, moisture observations, ingredient changes, compression conditions and speed. Compare with an acceptable campaign where available and label missing information instead of assuming conditions stayed constant.

16

Will increasing compression force always reduce sticking?

No. Research with some APIs found more sticking at higher pressure. Evaluate the response within approved conditions and check product quality as well as punch deposits before accepting a force change.

17

Can we compare dwell time using press RPM alone?

No. Press and tooling geometry also affect dwell time. Record the machine, head configuration and speed so a comparison does not assume that equal RPM means equal compression history.

18

Is there a universal moisture target for preventing sticking?

No. Define moisture measurements and any conditioning study for the formulation. Compare the same test method and sampling point, and retain storage, hold-time and environmental records.

19

Which punch coating is best for sticking and picking?

There is no universal choice. Evaluate the candidate with the formulation, tool material, existing treatment, dimensions and cleaning requirements. Verify release and surface condition over a representative run.

20

Does good powder flow rule out sticking?

No. Flow behavior alone does not establish punch release. Review available particle-property and material-change information alongside the tablet and tooling evidence.

21

What if filming improves but one engraved letter still picks?

Record the two outcomes separately. Investigate the remaining local feature while preserving the broader surface result, rather than declaring the complete problem solved from an overall reduction in rejected tablets.

22

Can we change the formulation, coating and engraving together?

A designed study may include multiple changes, but a combined improvement alone cannot establish which change caused it. State what the comparison can resolve and retain any uncertainty.

23

What should be included in a request for tooling evaluation?

Send paired tablet and punch photographs, tooling identity, drawing requirements, surface-treatment history and relevant production records. State whether the decision concerns surface recovery, engraving, coating or further process investigation.

24

What should a successful trial demonstrate beyond cleaner tooling?

It should address the original defect over comparable conditions and an appropriate observation period while preserving all required product attributes. Improvement in appearance alone cannot replace the responsible team's full acceptance requirements.

25

How should the outcome of a sticking-and-picking review be stated?

Identify the defect evaluated, change made, comparison used and observation period. Preserve remaining uncertainty and avoid extending a result to another formulation, machine or tooling configuration outside the actual review.
