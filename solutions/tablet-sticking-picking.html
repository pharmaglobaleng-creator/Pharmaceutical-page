Tablet Sticking vs Picking: Diagnose the Defect Before Rework

Browse 25 quick answers

A rough tablet face, a missing letter and a marked tablet band can need different corrective work. This comparison guide helps classify the defect, build a matched tablet-and-tool record and choose a focused investigation before changing the tooling.

Request a Tooling Evaluation

What is the difference between tablet sticking and picking?

Sticking is formulation adhering to the punch face. Picking is localized loss from the tablet where material is retained around punch detail, such as lettering or a logo. Record where the material remains and where it is missing; the names alone do not identify the cause.

Match the tablet defect to the tool surface

Photograph both tablet faces, the band and the corresponding punch before cleaning where procedures permit. Identify which face was formed by the upper or lower tool and keep the orientation consistent. A photograph of a damaged tablet without the punch identity cannot show whether the same local feature repeats at one station.

I Holland distinguishes sticking on the punch face from picking at punch detail. [1] Use the tablet sticking guide when a deposit spreads across a broader area. Use the tablet picking guide when material is repeatedly missing from a letter, logo or another identifiable feature.

If the main evidence is a marked tablet band or difficult release from the die, begin with die-wall binding. If damage appears only after transfer or coating, retain that sampling information. Do not label every missing tablet edge as picking when the place and time of damage are still unknown.

Use a comparison record that preserves the pattern

Collect acceptable and affected samples from identified points in the run. Record the press, product batch, tooling set, station information and run duration. Note the first observed defect and the sampling interval so that “started after an hour” is not mistaken for continuous observation when only occasional samples were taken.

Compare affected and unaffected tooling from the same campaign. Document cleaning history, coating identity, prior surface work and visible condition. A difference confined to one tool can direct the next inspection, but it does not prove that the tool is the only contributor. Likewise, widespread defects do not automatically exclude a tooling design issue.

Separate broad filming from localized picking in the record even when both occur on the same tablet. A change that improves the surrounding face may leave a damaged character unresolved. Counting both defects as one rejection category can hide that distinction and make an incomplete correction appear successful.

Give lettering and engraving a focused design review

For localized picking, identify the exact feature involved and compare it with the approved tool drawing. Record whether the retained material occurs at the same letter, narrow space or detail on several punches. Inspect the feature after approved cleaning so that a deposit is not mistaken for the underlying engraving shape.

Natoli's technical guidance discusses engraving design and identifier placement as contributors to picking. [2] Use that principle to request a drawing review, not to improvise a different logo with a polishing tool. Any proposed change should explain the feature being altered and how the required tablet identity and appearance will be preserved.

When the surface rather than the design is suspected, ask whether inspection supports recoverable damage and what dimensional allowance remains. Route that question to punch restoration. If the geometry is already outside its requirements, repeated polishing cannot recreate the missing material.

Investigate broad buildup through the whole run

Record whether the deposit is present at startup, develops gradually or returns soon after cleaning. Research using repeated compactions describes sticking as material accumulating on a punch over successive cycles. [3] A few acceptable tablets after restart therefore provide limited evidence about a problem that previously developed later.

Bring batch and process records into the same review: lubricant addition and mixing, recorded moisture observations, ingredient or supplier changes, compression conditions and press speed. Compare them with an acceptable campaign for the same product where possible. Note missing information explicitly instead of assuming that an unrecorded condition stayed constant.

Keep formulation decisions with the responsible formulation and quality team. This guide does not prescribe an extra lubricant percentage, a target moisture level or a new compression force. The useful first question is which recorded difference deserves a controlled study, and which product requirements must remain acceptable during that study.

Choose the intervention that addresses the evidence

A surface defect, an engraving feature and a formulation interaction are different reasons for intervention. For each proposed change, write down the observation it addresses. “Polish the set” is incomplete unless the scope identifies the recoverable surface condition and the measurements or geometry that must be protected.

A coating may help in a suitable application, but coating options should be evaluated against formulation, tool material and operating conditions. Natoli describes selection as dependent on the formulation and coating properties. [4] See the coating evaluation guide before choosing a treatment solely because another product used it.

When more than one mechanism is plausible, agree how the comparison will separate them. Changing the formulation, coating and engraving together may be an authorized development project, but its result cannot identify the contribution of each change without an appropriate study design. State what the trial can and cannot establish before it starts.

Define success beyond a clean startup

Agree the observation period and defect measures in advance. Record the time to first visible deposit, which punch area is involved, loss of tablet detail, rejected units and cleaning interruptions. Retain the required product-quality results alongside appearance. A lower rejection count is useful only when the sampling method and run conditions support the comparison.

Review broad sticking and local picking separately at the end of the trial. Document whether either remains, whether a new defect appeared and whether the surface or engraving changed during the work. When the original problem occurred late in a campaign, explain how the verification addresses that timing rather than declaring success after a short run.

For an evaluation request, send PharmaGlobalEng the paired tablet and punch photographs, tool identity, approved geometry, surface-treatment record and a brief history of previous trials. State the decision needed: surface recovery, engraving review, coating assessment or additional investigation. That focused request makes the next step clearer than asking for a universal anti-stick treatment.

Read the two tablet faces as separate evidence

Identify the upper and lower tablet faces before combining observations. A deposit on one punch and a localized defect on the opposite face may involve different tool features. Keep the photographs and descriptions paired with their corresponding tooling. If the tablet orientation was lost during collection, state that limitation instead of assigning a face by appearance alone.

Map the missing detail to the actual drawing. If the same character is affected repeatedly, compare its condition and intended geometry across identified punches. If defects occur at changing locations across the face, describe that distribution. Neither pattern proves a cause, but each helps choose where close inspection and a controlled comparison can provide useful evidence.

Avoid relying only on the most dramatic rejected tablet. Retain representative samples showing the usual defect as well as the worst example, with collection points. An isolated damaged tablet can overstate the frequency of one pattern while obscuring the defect responsible for most interruptions.

Illustrative comparison: broad film improves but lettering still picks

Imagine a hypothetical trial in which broad deposits become less visible after an agreed surface treatment, while the same letter still loses detail. Recording only the total number of rejected tablets may suggest that the problem is solved or may hide which defect improved. The record needs separate observations for face filming and local loss of detail.

For the remaining letter defect, inspect that feature and compare it with the drawing and acceptable tools. Identify whether retained material is present at the corresponding location. If the local feature was not inspected before treatment, that missing baseline limits what can be concluded about the treatment's effect on picking.

For the improved film, keep the observation period and operating conditions in view. If the trial ended before the original buildup usually appeared, the evidence is incomplete. If a comparable run shows sustained improvement, the result supports that specific outcome under the observed conditions; it does not demonstrate that the treatment will solve every formulation or engraving problem.

This example is illustrative, not a PharmaGlobalEng case history. Its purpose is to show how separate defect categories produce a more useful next decision: investigate the remaining local issue while preserving evidence about the broader surface response.

Check whether the evidence weakens the original theory

A good investigation includes observations that would make the proposed cause less likely. If the theory concerns a damaged engraving, compare the actual geometry and surface rather than relying solely on the defect's proximity to a logo. If the tooling is indistinguishable from acceptable examples within the inspection's limits, document that result and consider what other comparison is justified.

If the theory concerns a particular batch change, retain the relevant batch records and confirm the timing. A tool change or cleaning change made during the same period may prevent a simple attribution. Do not discard an inconvenient record because it does not fit the first explanation. The aim is a correction supported by the whole pattern.

An inconclusive comparison can still be useful. It may show that the sampling method cannot preserve station identity or that the surface condition was not recorded before cleaning. Define how the next investigation will close that gap. Repeating the same poorly documented trial adds activity without necessarily adding evidence.

Agree what the trial record will allow you to claim

Before a trial, distinguish its question from the broader production objective. One comparison may assess whether an identified surface can release the current product more consistently. Another may assess a proposed engraving change. Neither automatically qualifies a different machine, formulation or tooling configuration that was outside the agreed scope.

Retain both the improvement and any tradeoff. A cleaner face accompanied by unacceptable loss of tablet detail is not a successful picking correction. A reduced need for cleaning accompanied by another unacceptable product attribute also requires review. The responsible team defines the full acceptance requirements; the tooling record should make the relevant observations available.

Close the review with a specific statement about the defect and conditions evaluated. Identify what changed, the comparison used, how long the condition was observed and which question remains open. That language is more useful for future production decisions than declaring a coating or polishing method universally “anti-stick.”

25 practical questions and answers

Quick answers to the questions covered in this guide. Use the detailed sections above for the evidence and context behind each answer.

01

What distinction starts a sticking-versus-picking investigation?

Locate where material remains and where it is missing. Broad punch-face deposits indicate a different observation from localized loss around lettering or a logo, even when the two defects appear together.

02

Can sticking and picking occur on the same tablet?

Yes. Record broad deposits and localized loss separately so an improvement in one category does not conceal a remaining problem in the other during treatment selection or trial evaluation.

03

Why pair tablet photographs with the corresponding punches?

The pairing connects the visible defect with the actual tool feature and identity. Without it, a damaged tablet alone cannot establish whether the same local condition repeats at one station.

04

Which guide fits a broad film across the punch face?

Use the tablet sticking investigation for broad deposits and their progression. Retain timing, surface history and run conditions rather than treating the film as proof of a single formulation or tooling cause.

05

Which guide fits repeated loss around a tablet letter?

Use the tablet picking investigation and inspect the corresponding engraving. Compare retained material, surface condition and intended geometry before deciding whether a design review or recoverable surface work is justified.

06

What if the main problem is difficult ejection or band marking?

Begin with the die-wall binding investigation and retain the sampling stage. Do not automatically classify a sidewall mark or release problem as punch-face sticking without the corresponding evidence.

07

Should every missing tablet edge be called picking?

No. Establish where and when the damage appears and inspect the corresponding tooling. Missing material may be observed after another operation, so appearance alone is insufficient to classify the mechanism.

08

Which records preserve the timing of a sticking or picking defect?

Record the first observed defect, sampling interval, run duration and related operating conditions. Distinguish continuous observation from occasional samples so that the reported onset does not imply more precision than the evidence supports.

09

Why retain acceptable samples during a defect investigation?

Acceptable samples provide a comparison for the intended face and detail under identified conditions. Preserve collection points and tooling identity so they remain meaningful alongside affected samples and inspection results.

10

What should be compared between affected and unaffected punches?

Compare surface condition, dimensions, cleaning history, coating identity and prior work within the same campaign where practical. A localized difference guides the next inspection without excluding other contributors automatically.

11

How should localized picking around engraving be examined?

Identify the exact feature and compare it with the approved drawing after appropriate cleaning. Record whether the same location collects material across several tools, rather than treating every local defect as identical.

12

Can a polishing tool be used to improvise a different logo?

No. Changes to lettering or geometry require review and preservation of the required tablet identity. Reconditioning should not become an undocumented design change made simply to see whether the defect improves.

13

What should be checked before restoring a picking-related surface?

Establish that inspection supports recoverable damage and that sufficient allowance remains to retain the required geometry. Repeated polishing cannot recreate an engraving or profile already outside its requirements.

14

Why evaluate broad buildup over successive cycles?

Sticking can develop through accumulation during repeated compression. An acceptable brief restart therefore gives limited evidence about a defect that appeared later in the original production record.

15

Which formulation and process records belong in the comparison?

Retain lubricant addition and mixing, moisture observations, ingredient changes, compression conditions and speed. Compare with an acceptable campaign where available and label missing information instead of assuming conditions stayed constant.

16

Does the comparison guide prescribe a lubricant level or compression force?

No. Formulation and quality personnel define authorized studies and acceptance requirements. The guide helps identify which recorded differences deserve investigation without supplying universal operating adjustments for an unidentified product.

17

How should a proposed intervention be connected to the defect?

State the observation it addresses and the requirements it must preserve. A generic instruction to polish or coat is incomplete without the supported surface, geometry or formulation question behind that choice.

18

Why can simultaneous formulation, engraving and coating changes be inconclusive?

The combined project may improve the result without isolating each contribution. An appropriate study design and stated limitations are needed before assigning the improvement to a single treatment or feature.

19

Should upper and lower tablet faces be combined into one finding?

Keep them separate initially and retain their tooling identities. A deposit on one face and local damage on the other may involve different features, and lost orientation limits the comparison.

20

Why collect typical defects as well as the most severe example?

The worst rejected tablet may not represent the pattern responsible for most interruptions. Retain representative samples and their collection points so the investigation addresses distribution as well as severity.

21

What if filming improves but one engraved letter still picks?

Record the two outcomes separately. Investigate the remaining local feature while preserving the broader surface result, rather than declaring the complete problem solved from an overall reduction in rejected tablets.

22

How can evidence weaken an engraving-damage theory?

Compare the actual feature and surface with acceptable tools and the drawing. If the inspection does not support the proposed difference, document its limits and identify another justified comparison instead of retaining an unsupported diagnosis.

23

Why keep records that conflict with the first explanation?

A complete investigation must account for the whole pattern. Concurrent batch, tooling or cleaning changes can prevent a simple attribution, and conflicting observations help identify where the proposed cause remains uncertain.

24

What should a successful trial demonstrate beyond cleaner tooling?

It should address the original defect over comparable conditions and an appropriate observation period while preserving all required product attributes. Improvement in appearance alone cannot replace the responsible team's full acceptance requirements.

25

How should the outcome of a sticking-and-picking review be stated?

Identify the defect evaluated, change made, comparison used and observation period. Preserve remaining uncertainty and avoid extending a result to another formulation, machine or tooling configuration outside the actual review.
