Tablet Sticking and Picking: What to Check Before Changing the Tooling

We solve sticking and picking.

When a press keeps stopping because material is building up on the punches, another cleaning cycle is not much of a solution. The useful questions are where the material is staying behind, when it starts and what changed before the problem appeared.

Sticking and picking are not quite the same problem

Tablet sticking is material adhering to the punch face during compression. Picking is localized material loss from the tablet where product sticks to details on the punch, such as lettering or a logo.1

That distinction matters. A film spreading across the face and a defect repeating inside one letter should not be treated as identical observations. Photograph the tablet and the corresponding punch before cleaning, where your procedures permit it.

Start with the pattern, not a proposed fix

A useful starting point is to compare an affected punch with an unaffected punch from the same run. Record the station number, whether the upper or lower tooling is involved, and the time the defect first appeared.3

The checks below are starting points for an investigation, not diagnoses.

Punch-face filming: investigate the deposit before choosing a treatment

Here, filming means a layer of product building up on the punch face during compression—not the film-coating operation applied to finished tablets. Record where the layer begins, how it develops and whether it returns after the approved cleaning cycle.

Research into powder–tooling friction has identified punch-face film formation as a situation in which friction can change during tableting.13 That is a reason to document the developing surface condition, rather than assuming a clean tool behaves the same way throughout the run.

Suggested comparisons for recurring punch-face deposits
What to recordWhat the comparison helps establish
The first affected areaWhether the deposit begins across the face, near a particular detail or at selected stations.

Before- and after-cleaning photographsWhich observations concern removable product and which concern the underlying tool surface.
The batch and operating recordWhat was different between an acceptable run and the run with recurrent buildup.

A photograph alone cannot identify a deposit’s composition. If its identity affects the recommendation, ask the responsible team what analysis is needed. Do not label every residue as lubricant, coating failure or corrosion without supporting evidence.

Before selecting polishing or a coating, write down the intended result: a longer interval before buildup, less damage at a specified feature or a cleaner surface under an agreed inspection method. The target needs a comparable baseline and an appropriate observation period.

Choosing a magnesium stearate concentration is a formulation decision

There is no percentage recommended on this page. The cited research examined surface coverage together with concentration, drug loading, particle size and mixing conditions for two model drugs; it does not establish one concentration for every tablet.6

For a useful review, bring the lubricant grade, quantity, addition sequence and mixing record, together with the product’s approved requirements. Compare the affected batch with a successful one before proposing a change. Record a material or mixing change separately from any tooling work.

Ask the formulation and quality teams to define the permitted study and its acceptance checks. A cleaner punch face is not the only outcome that matters. PharmaGlobalEng’s tooling assessment should complement that work, not replace it or imply that we manufacture the formulation lubricant.

What to ask before sending a set for reconditioning

Prepare a short request stating the defect, the affected tools and the decision you need. For example: “Can the existing punch faces be assessed for recoverable damage while retaining the approved engraving?” Attach the drawing or inspection record when available.

Agree the cleaning and handling requirements, the inspection scope, the acceptance basis and the expected turnaround before shipping. Ask which findings would lead to reconditioning, further engineering review or replacement. This avoids treating a preliminary photograph review as a promise that every punch can be saved.

Use the polishing assessment guide for surface-work questions and the coating evaluation guide for treatment-selection questions. For damage that does not fit sticking or picking, start with the compression-defect comparison.

Check the punch surface properly

After documenting the defect, clean and dry the tooling using the approved method. Inspect the face under suitable lighting and magnification. Look for residue, scratches, pitting, corrosion and damaged areas that need closer assessment.4, 5

Controlled polishing may help with recoverable surface damage, but there has to be enough dimensional allowance to do the work. Cup geometry, engraving and other critical features still need to meet their requirements afterward. A cracked punch is not a polishing job.5

Also, a mirror finish is not proof that a punch will release a particular formulation. Some anti-stick surfaces are deliberately textured. Judge the surface against its specification and application, not shine alone.12

Look at the blend and the run conditions

Moisture and batch history. Compare the problem batch with a successful run. Review the recorded moisture result, granulation observations, storage conditions and any changes to ingredients or processing. Those records help decide what deserves a controlled investigation; they do not justify adjusting a batch by guesswork.3

Lubrication. Check the lubricant addition and mixing record before deciding to add more. Research on magnesium stearate shows that sticking behavior can depend on lubricant coverage, active-ingredient properties and mixing conditions. The amount added is only part of the picture.6

Compression conditions. Record force and press speed together with tablet quality. Experimental work has shown that compression pressure and speed can change sticking behavior, but its results should not be treated as a universal press-setting recipe. Keep trials within the approved process or an authorized study.7

Changing several things at once can leave you with a better-looking tablet and no clear explanation. For an initial troubleshooting trial, define the question, document the baseline and make a controlled change. Use a planned study when interactions between variables need to be assessed.

For picking, give the engraving its own inspection

When product keeps collecting in the same detail, inspect that area specifically rather than treating the whole face as one surface. Compare the lettering, score line or logo with its approved geometry, and look for a difference between affected and unaffected tools.1, 8

Do not deepen an engraving, round an edge or remove extra material simply to see whether the tablet improves. Design changes need engineering review. Polishing should preserve the intended detail, not gradually turn it into a different shape.5, 8

Where polishing and coatings can help

A suitable coating can be part of the answer when the formulation and tooling surface are not releasing well together. But coatings are not interchangeable. The formulation, tooling material, environment and process all matter when selecting one.9

At PharmaGlobalEng, you can discuss punch polishing, surface engineering and coating evaluation with us. Send the tooling details and defect evidence before choosing a treatment. The review needs to consider the existing surface, geometry, cleaning method and operating conditions.5, 10

A recommendation should explain what problem the treatment is intended to address and how its performance will be checked. A coating name on its own does not answer either question.

Make sure the improvement lasts

A short restart can look good even when material later builds up again. Compare the trial over a relevant run period, with enough information to judge repeatability.2

For a practical trial record, note the time to the first defect, where it appeared, rejected tablets, cleaning stops and the operating conditions. Agree the acceptance criteria before the trial.
