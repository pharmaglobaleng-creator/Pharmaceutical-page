Confirm that the problem is punch-face sticking

Tablet sticking is material adhering to the punch face during compression. Picking is localized material loss from the tablet where product sticks to details on the punch, such as lettering or a logo.1

Use the sticking-versus-picking comparison if the defect classification is uncertain. A film spreading across the face and a defect repeating inside one letter should not be treated as identical observations. Photograph the tablet and the corresponding punch before cleaning, where your procedures permit it.

Start with the pattern, not a proposed fix

A useful starting point is to compare an affected punch with an unaffected punch from the same run. Record the station number, whether the upper or lower tooling is involved, and the time the defect first appeared.3

Punch-face filming: investigate the deposit before choosing a treatment

Here, filming means a layer of product building up on the punch face during compression—not the film-coating operation applied to finished tablets. Record where the layer begins, how it develops and whether it returns after the approved cleaning cycle.

Research into powder–tooling friction has identified punch-face film formation as a situation in which friction can change during tableting.13 That is a reason to document the developing surface condition, rather than assuming a clean tool behaves the same way throughout the run.

A photograph alone cannot identify a deposit’s composition. If its identity affects the recommendation, ask the responsible team what analysis is needed. Do not label every residue as lubricant, coating failure or corrosion without supporting evidence.

Before selecting polishing or a coating, write down the intended result: a longer interval before buildup, less damage at a specified feature or a cleaner surface under an agreed inspection method. The target needs a comparable baseline and an appropriate observation period.

Choosing a magnesium stearate concentration is a formulation decision

There is no percentage recommended on this page. The cited research examined surface coverage together with concentration, drug loading, particle size and mixing conditions for two model drugs; it does not establish one concentration for every tablet.6

For a useful review, bring the lubricant grade, quantity, addition sequence and mixing record, together with the product’s approved requirements. Compare the affected batch with a successful one before proposing a change. Record a material or mixing change separately from any tooling work.

Ask the formulation and quality teams to define the permitted study and its acceptance checks. A cleaner punch face is not the only outcome that matters. PharmaGlobalEng’s tooling assessment should complement that work, not replace it or imply that we manufacture the formulation lubricant.

What to ask before sending a set for reconditioning

Prepare a short request stating the defect, the affected tools and the decision you need. For example: “Can the existing punch faces be assessed for recoverable damage while retaining the approved engraving?” Attach the drawing or inspection record when available.

Agree the cleaning and handling requirements, the inspection scope, the acceptance basis and the expected turnaround before shipping. Ask which findings would lead to reconditioning, further engineering review or replacement. This avoids treating a preliminary photograph review as a promise that every punch can be saved.

Use the polishing assessment guide for surface-work questions and the coating evaluation guide for treatment-selection questions. For damage that does not fit sticking or picking, start with the compression-defect comparison.

Check the punch surface properly

After documenting the defect, clean and dry the tooling using the approved method. Inspect the face under suitable lighting and magnification. Look for residue, scratches, pitting, corrosion and damaged areas that need closer assessment.4, 5

Controlled polishing may help with recoverable surface damage, but there has to be enough dimensional allowance to do the work. Cup geometry, engraving and other critical features still need to meet their requirements afterward. A cracked punch is not a polishing job.5

Also, a mirror finish is not proof that a punch will release a particular formulation. Some anti-stick surfaces are deliberately textured. Judge the surface against its specification and application, not shine alone.12

Record the time between surface preparation and the trial

A useful trial record starts before the first compression. Record how the punch was cleaned, whether it was polished or coated, the drying and storage steps, and the time between preparation and use. “Same punches” is not enough when one comparison follows cleaning immediately and another uses a stored set.

A 2026 study on S7 steel found that surface preparation and subsequent exposure to ambient conditions changed sticking behavior for the materials studied. The authors highlighted the need to control the cleaning-to-testing interval.14 This supports documenting surface history; it does not establish a production hold time or justify deliberately exposing cleaned tooling outside the approved procedure.

Include these preparation details when comparing a new coating with an existing surface. Otherwise, a difference in handling may be attributed to the coating without sufficient evidence. Keep the comparison tied to the actual product, tool condition and run period, and record remaining uncertainty.

Look at the blend and the run conditions

Moisture and batch history. Compare the problem batch with a successful run. Review the recorded moisture result, granulation observations, storage conditions and any changes to ingredients or processing. Those records help decide what deserves a controlled investigation; they do not justify adjusting a batch by guesswork.3

Lubrication. Check the lubricant addition and mixing record before deciding to add more. Research on magnesium stearate shows that sticking behavior can depend on lubricant coverage, active-ingredient properties and mixing conditions. The amount added is only part of the picture.6

Compression conditions. Record force and press speed together with tablet quality. Experimental work has shown that compression pressure and speed can change sticking behavior, but its results should not be treated as a universal press-setting recipe. Keep trials within the approved process or an authorized study.7

Changing several things at once can leave you with a better-looking tablet and no clear explanation. For an initial troubleshooting trial, define the question, document the baseline and make a controlled change. Use a planned study when interactions between variables need to be assessed.

For picking, give the engraving its own inspection

When product keeps collecting in the same detail, inspect that area specifically rather than treating the whole face as one surface. Compare the lettering, score line or logo with its approved geometry, and look for a difference between affected and unaffected tools.1, 8

Do not deepen an engraving, round an edge or remove extra material simply to see whether the tablet improves. Design changes need engineering review. Polishing should preserve the intended detail, not gradually turn it into a different shape.5, 8

Where polishing and coatings can help

A suitable coating can be part of the answer when the formulation and tooling surface are not releasing well together. But coatings are not interchangeable. The formulation, tooling material, environment and process all matter when selecting one.9

At PharmaGlobalEng, you can discuss punch polishing, surface engineering and coating evaluation with us. Send the tooling details and defect evidence before choosing a treatment. The review needs to consider the existing surface, geometry, cleaning method and operating conditions.5, 10

A recommendation should explain what problem the treatment is intended to address and how its performance will be checked. A coating name on its own does not answer either question.

Make sure the improvement lasts

A short restart can look good even when material later builds up again. Compare the trial over a relevant run period, with enough information to judge repeatability.2

For a practical trial record, note the time to the first defect, where it appeared, rejected tablets, cleaning stops and the operating conditions. Agree the acceptance criteria before the trial. Your manufacturing and quality teams remain responsible for process changes and return-to-production approval.5

Granulation moisture and tablet sticking: compare the right evidence

The moisture result on a batch record and the conditions at the punch face answer different questions. A bulk sample describes the material sampled at a stated point; it does not directly measure water at the tablet–punch interface throughout compression. Published experimental work on moisture during tableting examines how temperature and moisture behavior evolve during a run. This makes the timing and method of a moisture comparison important, rather than making one isolated result a diagnosis.

Compare the affected granulation with a satisfactory reference using the same approved measurement method and a clearly identified sampling point. Retain the sample time, any hold before compression and the relevant storage or transfer history. If the two results use different methods or come from different stages, resolve that difference before treating a numerical gap as a production explanation.

Keep the tooling review connected to the comparison. Record when adhesion first appears and whether it begins on a particular face region or across the set. Local scratches or damaged details still deserve inspection even when material history has changed. Conversely, polishing a face does not establish that the granulation or its handling meets the product requirements. PharmaGlobalEng can assess the tooling contribution while your formulation and manufacturing teams investigate the material question.

Excipients, magnesium stearate and punch adhesion

Searching for excipients to prevent tablet sticking raises a formulation question as well as a tooling question. The useful comparison identifies the ingredient grade, addition stage and mixing history involved. Separate a formulation lubricant blended into the product from an external lubricant applied to tooling, and separate both from a lubricant intended for machine contacts. The same general word does not make these uses interchangeable.

Magnesium stearate is not an automatic correction for every sticking system. Xiang and Sun reported that external magnesium-stearate lubrication aggravated sticking under some low-pressure conditions in their experimental work. The finding does not define a setting for your product; it shows why the application and method of lubrication matter. Keep any proposed ingredient or process change within the responsible formulation and quality review.

For a tooling enquiry, explain whether the problem began after an ingredient, grade or blending change and whether the same punch set previously ran satisfactorily. If powder remains on an otherwise acceptable face, discuss surface compatibility as well as visible damage. If there are localized scratches, corrosion or engraving defects, identify those separately. The aim is to select a justified tooling service without presenting a polishing or coating job as a replacement for formulation development.

Technical source: Xiang and Sun, Worsened punch sticking by external lubrication with magnesium stearate (2024).

Tablet tooling cup depth and sticking

Cup depth belongs in a review of the full punch-face design. The relevant question is where product adheres relative to the curvature, perimeter and engraved detail. A deposit concentrated beside a letter may need a different investigation from buildup across a broad cup surface. Photograph the complete face before magnifying the affected area so that its position remains clear.

Compare the observed tooling with the approved drawing and a suitable reference punch. A different cup profile changes more than one isolated dimension, so a proposal to make the cup shallower should explain the complete geometry change and the tablet requirements affected. Do not use an abrasive to alter the cup as an informal trial.

For a design-specific concern, use our tablet tooling design and picking review. For local texture or scratches, use the surface-roughness and Microfinish guide. These routes help define which aspect of the tool needs attention while preserving the approved geometry.

Illustrative comparison: a clean restart is followed by filming

Consider a hypothetical run in which the first collected tablets look acceptable, but a film later develops on the upper punch faces. Cleaning restores the initial appearance and the pattern then repeats. That sequence establishes recurrence after cleaning; it does not by itself show whether the dominant contributor is the surface, the formulation or a condition that changes during the run.

Compare the affected surface at startup and at the first recorded buildup. Retain the elapsed production time, preparation history and corresponding tablet samples. If only the final heavy deposit is photographed, the investigation loses information about where the film began and how it spread. Record whether the same pattern appears across the set or only on identified tools.

Now suppose a proposed treatment is evaluated over a much shorter run. An acceptable result during that interval cannot answer whether the later filming has been prevented. The verification needs a justified observation window and comparable conditions, with the relevant product-quality checks. A trial's duration is part of the evidence rather than an incidental scheduling detail.

This example illustrates how to interpret a repeating pattern; it is not a PharmaGlobalEng customer case or a promise of a particular treatment outcome. The appropriate next study depends on the actual tooling and production records.

Distinguish what a surface trial changed

Record the identity of the treated tools and the work actually performed. “Polished and coated” combines two changes and may leave unclear which surface condition was assessed first. Keep the pre-treatment inspection, protected geometry, final inspection and preparation record together so the trial can be interpreted against the actual tool condition.

If both treated and comparison tools are used in an authorized study, record how their positions and exposure differ. A difference between groups is difficult to interpret when one group had a different cleaning history or observation period. Explain such limitations rather than presenting every improvement as proof of the treatment's independent effect.

When the result is inconclusive, state which question remains unanswered: whether the surface met its intended specification, whether filming was observed long enough, or whether process differences prevent a fair comparison. The next action should resolve that question. Repeating the treatment without improving the evidence may leave the same uncertainty after another interrupted campaign.

25 practical questions and answers

Quick answers to the questions covered in this guide. Use the detailed sections above for the evidence and context behind each answer.

Recognize sticking and picking

01

What is tablet sticking?

Tablet sticking is formulation adhering to the punch face during compression. Identify the deposit location and progression, then compare the corresponding tablets and tooling before choosing a surface or process intervention.

02

How does picking differ from broad punch-face sticking?

Picking is localized material loss associated with retained product around details such as lettering or a logo. A broader film and a repeatedly damaged character require separate observations even when both occur together.

03

Does a clean startup rule out a sticking problem?

No. Material can accumulate over repeated compression cycles. Retain the time to onset and compare a relevant run period rather than treating the appearance of the first tablets as a complete evaluation.

04

What should be compared when only a few stations show buildup?

Compare affected and unaffected tools from the same run, retaining station identity, dimensions, condition and service history. The distribution guides inspection but does not establish a tooling-only cause by itself.

05

Do chipped tablet-edge photographs prove picking?

No. Missing edge material shows a defect but does not establish its cause. The sampling stage and corresponding punch condition are needed before classifying the damage as picking or another mechanism.

Inspect and assess the punch surface

06

Why photograph deposits before cleaning the punches?

The photographs preserve where material collected and how it was distributed. Follow approved procedures and pair those observations with the cleaned surface so that residue is not confused with underlying damage.

07

What should be inspected after the tooling is cleaned and dried?

Inspect the actual face under suitable lighting and magnification for residue, scratches, pitting, corrosion and other damage. Compare findings with the applicable surface and geometry requirements before selecting corrective work.

08

Can polishing improve every sticking problem?

No. Controlled polishing may help recoverable surface damage when dimensions and geometry can be preserved. It does not automatically resolve formulation or process contributors and is not a repair for cracked tooling.

09

Does a mirror finish guarantee formulation release?

No. Shine alone does not establish how a surface will interact with the actual formulation. Judge the finish against its specification and application, then evaluate performance through an appropriate comparison.

Review formulation and press records

10

Which batch records help investigate sticking?

Compare the affected and successful runs using recorded moisture observations, ingredients, processing, storage and relevant changes. The records identify questions for controlled study rather than justify guessing a batch adjustment.

11

What information is needed before changing magnesium stearate concentration?

Retain lubricant grade, quantity, addition sequence and mixing history alongside product requirements. A universal concentration cannot be selected from a sticking complaint because formulation and processing conditions influence the comparison.

12

Why record compression force and press speed together?

Both belong in the run history and may affect the observed response. Evaluate them alongside tablet quality within the approved process or an authorized study rather than applying a universal setting recipe.

Inspect engraving and select treatments

13

What makes a useful engraving inspection for localized picking?

Identify the exact detail involved and compare affected and unaffected tools with the approved geometry. Retain evidence of product collection and surface condition instead of treating the whole face as one undifferentiated surface.

14

Should engraving be deepened or rounded to test whether sticking improves?

No. Geometry changes require engineering review and preservation of intended tablet detail. Uncontrolled removal of material can turn a reconditioning attempt into an undocumented change of the tool design.

15

How should a coating be selected for a sticking application?

Consider the formulation, tooling material, existing surface, cleaning environment and process. The recommendation should explain which observed issue the treatment addresses and how its performance will be evaluated.

16

Can one coating be assumed to suit every formulation?

No. Coating properties and formulation interactions differ. A successful result for another product does not establish suitability for the current tooling and operating conditions without an appropriate application review.

Prepare a service request and assess recurrence

17

Why record the interval between cleaning and a sticking trial?

Surface preparation and subsequent exposure can affect sticking behavior in research comparisons. Retain the preparation-to-use interval so an unexplained handling difference is not attributed solely to the tool treatment.

18

Does surface-aging research establish a production hold time?

No. The cited S7 steel study supports recording and controlling surface history in comparisons. It does not prescribe a universal hold time or authorize exposure outside the approved tooling procedure.

19

What should accompany a punch reconditioning enquiry?

Describe the defect, affected tools and decision needed, with drawings or inspection records where available. Agree protected features, assessment scope, acceptance basis and handling requirements before shipping the set.

20

Does filming that returns after cleaning identify its cause?

No. It establishes recurrence after cleaning, but does not distinguish surface, formulation and changing run conditions by itself. Compare where the film starts, its timing and the relevant preparation and production records.

21

Why preserve early buildup observations rather than only the worst deposit?

The initial pattern can show where material began collecting and how it spread. A photograph taken only after heavy buildup may conceal information needed to compare tools or evaluate the progression.

22

Can a short treatment trial confirm prevention of late-run filming?

Not by itself. The trial needs a justified observation window and comparable conditions that address the original onset pattern, together with the required product-quality checks and recorded limitations.

Interpret treatment trials

23

What does a combined polishing-and-coating trial fail to isolate?

It combines more than one surface change. Preserve the pre-treatment inspection and work history, and avoid claiming an independent coating effect when the comparison cannot distinguish it from the other changes.

24

How should treated and comparison tools be documented?

Retain identities, positions, preparation histories and exposure periods. Differences in handling or observation can complicate interpretation, so explain them before attributing the outcome solely to the proposed treatment.

25

What should an inconclusive sticking trial lead to?

Identify the unanswered question, such as surface conformity, observation duration or process comparability. The next action should close that evidence gap rather than repeat treatment without improving the basis for interpretation.
