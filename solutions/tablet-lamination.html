Tablet Lamination: Investigate the Separation Before Changing the Tooling

When tablets split into layers, keep the fracture evidence and the process history together. The next step should follow what happened during the run—not an automatic increase in compression force or an order for replacement punches.

Describe the separation precisely

Lamination is splitting within the tablet body into layers. Capping involves separation of a crown. Use the visible pattern to describe the defect before assigning a cause.[1]

Photograph the tablet from the side as well as from the faces. Record whether the split is complete, whether several planes are visible and whether the pattern repeats across samples. Preserve the sample orientation and the point at which it was collected.

Separate observations from interpretation. “A horizontal split became visible after transfer” is an observation. “The die trapped air” is a proposed explanation that still needs evidence. Keeping those statements separate makes the investigation easier to review and prevents an early assumption from deciding the repair.

For a deliberately multilayer tablet, also identify whether the separation is at a designed layer interface. That question belongs in the product-specific investigation rather than being treated as identical to a split in a single-layer compact.

Do not treat every laminated tablet as the same mechanism

Air-entrapment hypothesis. A 2024 study used in-die elastic-recovery measurements to optimize precompression for a model blend with air-entrapment-related lamination.[2] This supports investigating the precompression stage when the evidence fits; it is not a universal pressure setting for production.

Unloading hypothesis. Research on a compaction simulator found that changing unloading conditions changed the occurrence of capping and lamination.[3] Keep information about the press and the compression cycle available to the engineer reviewing the problem.

Layer-interface hypothesis. In a study of one bilayer formulation, internal cracking and ejection-related stresses were investigated together.[4] The appropriate assessment depends on the product; do not transfer a bilayer result into a universal explanation for all tablets.

The purpose of identifying a hypothesis is to decide what observation would support or challenge it. Avoid collecting a large file of unrelated readings without stating which question each one helps answer.

Compare the affected run with a suitable baseline

Use a successful run of the same product and tooling design where available. Record differences openly when the baseline is not directly comparable. The following is a suggested investigation worksheet, not a list of mandatory process settings.

Keep the raw observations where possible, not only a conclusion such as “pressure normal.” Identify the instrument or record used and any missing data. A comparison is weaker when a setting was recalled later rather than captured during the run.

Review die geometry without guessing a taper or clearance

A 2026 study examined die taper, punch–die clearance and compaction position using two formulations. The effects differed between those formulations.[5] That is a useful warning against treating a tapered die as an automatic answer.

Send the existing die specification and approved punch drawings for engineering review. Record whether the problem started with a tooling change, whether the nominal design is the same and whether service work has altered the set’s condition. Keep dimensional checks traceable to the actual tools.

Do not machine a die, enlarge a clearance or change punch penetration from a general web recommendation. Any proposal must address compatibility, the required geometry, equipment limits and the approved process. Where the review needs the original equipment or tooling manufacturer, include that requirement in the plan.

PharmaGlobalEng’s role is to discuss the tooling condition and whether an inspection, polishing or surface-engineering route is appropriate. We should not recommend surface work when the evidence instead points to a different part of the process.

When reconditioning may be worth evaluating

Reconditioning is a possible route only when the identified condition is recoverable and the tool can meet its requirements afterward. Ask what feature will be worked, how it will be checked and what would rule the tool out.

Replacing a set without investigating the mechanism may leave the same unanswered questions. Equally, keeping a damaged tool in service because replacement takes time is not a sound decision. Compare the technical suitability of each route before its cost or lead time.

A useful request: “Please review whether the observed tooling condition could justify reconditioning, and identify the measurements needed before a decision.” This asks for evidence and scope rather than a guarantee that polishing will cure the defect.

Verify the change across the relevant stages

Define the test question, baseline, sampling stages and observation period before starting. Agree what counts as lamination and how affected samples will be recorded. Include product-specific testing and any later stage where the original damage was first observed.

Record each approved change. When a tooling intervention and a process change are both necessary, explain why the trial cannot isolate their individual contributions. A successful combined trial may be useful without proving that either change would work alone.

Do not close the investigation solely because no split was visible at the press discharge during a brief check. State which stages and periods were assessed, what the observations showed and what remains uncertain. Acceptance and release decisions belong to the manufacturer’s responsible teams.

For future campaigns, retain the tooling identity, work performed, comparison record and follow-up requirements. This gives the next investigation a usable baseline instead of starting again with an unexplained replacement order.

Questions about tablets splitting into layers

Will more precompression always stop lamination?

No universal setting is established here. The relevant evidence, product behavior and press configuration need to be reviewed, and any proposed trial must remain within an authorized plan.

Is a laminated tablet proof of poor tooling?

No. Tooling condition is one part of the evidence. Compare the fracture pattern, timing and process history before choosing between tooling work and other corrective actions.

Can photographs establish that a tablet is sound internally?

A surface photograph records what is visible. It cannot answer every question about an internal defect. The responsible technical and quality teams should specify any additional assessment needed for the product.

Bring the fracture evidence to the tooling review

Send PharmaGlobalEng side-view photographs, the press and tooling details, the sampling stage and a summary of the affected run. Include the changes already attempted and their results.
