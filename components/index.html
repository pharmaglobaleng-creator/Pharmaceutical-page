Identify the complete assembly before selecting a part

A component name is a starting point, not a compatibility specification. Similar-looking tooling can differ in dimensions, orientation, material, revision, or mating features. Record the manufacturer and model of the machine, the assembly location, the marking on the existing component, and any drawing reference. Include the relevant manual page when available. If a number comes from a packaging label rather than the component itself, make that distinction in the enquiry.

Photograph the complete assembly before removal when site procedures allow it, then photograph the separate component from several directions. Preserve information about adjacent washers, spacers, fasteners, and handed features. The purpose is to show what the part does in its installed position. A close-up of a worn edge is useful for condition assessment but rarely establishes all the information needed to identify a replacement.

Tablet punches: geometry, identification, and condition

Tablet punches form the tablet faces and transmit the compression load through the tooling system. Their working features include the tip and cup, any engraving or score detail, the barrel, and the head that interacts with the press. Upper and lower punches should retain their identities throughout inspection and maintenance. For shaped tooling, record orientation features and their relationship to the tablet design; a product image alone does not define the tool.

Review damage by location. Material attached to the cup, rounding at an edge, marks along the barrel, and damage at the head are different observations. Record whether a feature is repeated across the set or limited to a station. Applicable dimensions and working-length relationships must come from the approved tooling specification; appearance cannot establish conformity. Use tooling inspection to define a measurement review and tablet compression tooling guidance for application and specification questions.

A suspected crack requires a separate integrity decision. Keep the tool identified and follow the site’s isolation procedure instead of attempting to hide the line by polishing. The punch cracking guide explains the evidence needed for that investigation.

Tablet dies: the bore and its relationship to the punch

The die provides the cavity in which material is filled and compressed and through which the tablet is ejected. Assess the bore and entry alongside the associated punches. Record visible scoring, deposits, wear patterns, or damage without assuming they all have the same cause. Measurements need a defined location and method: an unexplained single diameter does not describe variation through a worn bore.

When the complaint involves difficult release, preserve the tablets and note any sidewall marks or changes in ejection behavior. Compare the affected station with an unaffected station using the same product and operating period when that comparison is available. The tablet binding guide deals with die-wall resistance and ejection symptoms; die maintenance addresses inspection and care. These routes answer different questions and may both be relevant before a replacement is specified.

Feed frames and filling components

Feed-frame components are part of the system that presents powder or granulation to the die-filling area. Start an enquiry with the feeder assembly identity, machine model, and the exact location of the component. Record the position and orientation of paddles, scraper elements, seals, and other relevant features against the machine documentation. Do not infer that a part fits from a photograph of another machine in the same family.

For a filling complaint, record whether weight variation appeared after a product change, component change, cleaning, or reassembly. Note the available feeder and press settings together with the sample timing. A loose observation that the feeder “looks worn” is less useful than a photograph and a comparison to the specified feature. The tablet weight variation guide helps organize this evidence. Installation clearances and adjustments remain specific to the machine and its instructions.

Capsule-machine component enquiries

A capsule-machine enquiry needs the machine identity and the capsule format or size associated with the assembly. Include the exact component name from the manual, its reference number, drawing revision if known, and photographs of the installed and removed item. Explain whether the request concerns identification, visible damage, an assembly problem, or a proposed replacement. A generic description such as “capsule part” leaves several different machine functions unresolved.

Record the relationship between the component and its mating assembly. If only a broken fragment is available, identify which dimensions are missing rather than reconstructing them by guesswork. Send the component information for review so scope and availability can be confirmed. This reference page is not a declaration that every capsule-machine part or format is available from stock.

Separate component condition from replacement compatibility

Inspection asks whether the existing component meets its requirements. Identification asks which component is required. Keep both questions visible when ordering after a failure. A correctly identified replacement can fail again if the surrounding assembly still has an unresolved problem; a sound-looking item can also be unsuitable if its specification is wrong for the application.

For worn tooling, collect the previous service history and relevant operating observations before deciding between maintenance, restoration, and replacement. Where the cause remains uncertain, an evaluation can define the next evidence needed. Do not treat a surface treatment as a substitute for confirming dimensions and integrity.

Use the replacement-parts catalog with supporting information

The parts store organizes listed components by machine family and available reference information. For example, the Korsch 300 catalog provides a model-specific starting point. Use the catalog identity in your enquiry and attach the information from the actual machine. Where an OEM reference is absent or compatibility has not been confirmed, leave that question open for review.

Build a quotation request around the required quantity, machine model, component reference, and relevant drawing or photograph. Compatibility, availability, lead time, and pricing require confirmation before purchase. If the required item is not listed, send the identification details rather than choosing the nearest-looking substitute. Keeping these details together makes it easier to resolve an enquiry without losing the connection between the component, its application, and the original problem.

Prepare measurements that can be interpreted

A useful dimension has a named feature, a unit, a method, and a reference. Mark the measured feature on a drawing or annotated photograph so another person can tell where the reading came from. Distinguish an overall dimension from a working dimension and identify the surfaces used as references. A measurement without that context may be accurate yet answer the wrong compatibility question.

Record the instrument used and the condition of the component when measured. Deposits, burrs, missing sections, or visible deformation can limit what a reading represents. Do not describe an estimate from an image as a measured dimension. If only approximate information is available for initial identification, label it accordingly and identify which details still need confirmation before manufacture, purchase, or installation.

Where the drawing and the existing part disagree, preserve both records. The fitted component could have been reworked, replaced with a different revision, or measured at a different feature. None of those explanations should be accepted without evidence. Ask which specification governs the intended application and whether a controlled change exists. Copying the dimensions of an unidentified worn item can reproduce its uncertainty along with its shape.

Resolve conflicting names, numbers, and revisions

Catalog titles often describe a function, while a machine manual identifies an assembly and a drawing identifies a particular revision. Keep those references in separate fields. A PharmaGlobalEng catalog identifier is not automatically an OEM number. If the existing part carries a marking that differs from the manual, provide both and explain where each was found. This helps distinguish a cross-reference question from a simple transcription error.

For a matched assembly, record which members are being retained and which are being replaced. Identify any change in handedness, mounting position, locating feature, or adjacent component. A part can share a familiar name while belonging to a different assembly arrangement. When a catalog entry is only a possible match, use it to begin the enquiry rather than presenting it as confirmed compatibility.

An illustrative example is a feed-frame item whose stamped reference differs from the maintenance record. The useful next step is to reconcile the installed assembly, manual illustration, and component photographs. Ordering solely from either number leaves the discrepancy unresolved. Another example is a punch with an unreadable identity: the tablet shape may narrow the search, but it does not establish the full tooling specification or service history.

Check the incoming component against the agreed request

Before installation, compare the supplied item and documentation with the confirmed order and applicable specification. Verify the identifier, quantity, revision where relevant, and any agreed dimensional or material records. Keep packaging labels associated with the item until the receiving checks are complete. If a difference is found, document it before modifying the component to make it fit.

Use the machine manufacturer’s instructions and the site’s authorized setup procedure for installation. This guide does not supply universal clearances, torque values, or assembly adjustments. The important information for a technical enquiry is the actual difference observed, the feature involved, and the reference against which it was assessed. Photographs of both mating features can help explain a fit concern without treating an improvised adjustment as the answer.

After an approved replacement, connect the new component identity to the maintenance record and the original reason for replacement. Follow-up should ask whether the reported symptom has changed under comparable conditions. If the same damage reappears, retain the new observations alongside the original ones. A documented recurrence can direct the next investigation toward the assembly or operating conditions instead of simply repeating the same order.

25 practical questions and answers

Quick answers to the questions covered in this guide. Use the detailed sections above for the evidence and context behind each answer.

01

What is the first step in identifying a component?

Record the machine identity, assembly location, existing marking, and available manual or drawing reference.

02

Does a similar appearance prove compatibility?

No. Dimensions, orientation, material, revision, and mating features can differ between similar-looking components.

03

Why photograph the installed assembly?

It preserves the part’s position and relationship to adjacent components before removal changes that context.

04

Which information should accompany a close-up of damage?

Include an overview, component identity, damage location, and the relevant machine or assembly reference.

05

What do tablet punches do?

Punches form the tablet faces and transmit compression load through the tooling system.

06

Which punch features may need identification?

The tip, cup, engraving or score detail, barrel, head, and any orientation or locating features relevant to the design.

07

Why keep upper and lower punches distinguishable?

Their roles, identities, and service histories need to remain traceable during inspection, maintenance, and setup.

08

Does tablet shape define the full punch specification?

No. Shape is only part of the required tooling information; the complete drawing and machine application still matter.

09

Where should a suspected punch crack be routed?

Keep the tool identified and follow the site’s isolation and investigation process. Polishing is not a structural repair.

10

What does the tablet die provide?

It provides the cavity used for filling and compression and the passage through which the tablet is ejected.

11

Why can one bore measurement be insufficient?

A single unexplained diameter does not show where it was taken or describe variation through a worn bore.

12

Which tablet observations can help a die review?

Sidewall marks, difficult release, station patterns, and documented ejection behavior can help focus the investigation.

13

What information identifies a feed-frame component?

The machine and feeder assembly identity, exact component location, manual reference, orientation, and relevant photographs.

14

Does feeder wear alone prove the cause of weight variation?

No. The condition needs to be reviewed alongside sampling, material supply, settings, and the timing of the reported change.

15

What additional identity matters for capsule-machine enquiries?

Include the capsule format or size associated with the assembly, alongside the machine, component, and drawing information.

16

What is the difference between condition and compatibility?

Condition concerns whether an existing item meets requirements; compatibility concerns whether a proposed item is correct for the application.

17

What makes a dimension interpretable?

Name the feature, unit, method, and reference surfaces, ideally using an annotated drawing or photograph.

18

Is a dimension estimated from a photograph a measured specification?

No. Label image-based estimates as approximate and identify what requires confirmation before purchase, manufacture, or installation.

19

What should happen when a drawing and fitted part disagree?

Preserve both records and resolve the governing specification, revision, and possible service history before selecting a replacement.

20

Why distinguish a catalog identifier from an OEM reference?

They serve different identification roles. Treating them as identical can create a false cross-reference.

21

Why retain different numbers found on the part and manual?

Their discrepancy may reveal a revision, assembly, or transcription issue that needs resolution.

22

What should be recorded for a partially replaced assembly?

Identify the retained and replaced members and any relevant handedness, locating features, mounting positions, or revisions.

23

What should receiving checks compare?

Compare the delivered identity, quantity, relevant revision, and agreed records against the confirmed request and specification.

24

Does this guide supply universal installation settings?

No. Clearances, adjustments, and installation requirements depend on the machine instructions and authorized site procedure.

25

What should be retained if the same damage returns after replacement?

Keep the new identity and observations with the original failure record to support investigation of the assembly or operating conditions.
