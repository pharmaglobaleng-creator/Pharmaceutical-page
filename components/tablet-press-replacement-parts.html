Parts support for the equipment you operate

When production needs a replacement component, the key question is not only “what part is this?” It is “which revision and configuration does this press require?” An incorrect cross-reference can waste time and create a mismatch at installation. Preserve the relationship between the failed component, the machine, and the assembly before ordering.

Fette tablet press spare parts

If the part number is missing or conflicts across records, provide both references and identify where each was found.

Kilian tablet press parts

Manesty tablet press parts

Sejong tablet press parts

Common tablet press components to identify

Enquiries may involve tablet punches and dies, feed frames, compression rollers, scraper blades, or other press assembly components. The component name alone is not enough to establish a match: note its position, interfaces, orientation, relevant material or finish requirements, and relationship to adjacent parts as shown in the machine documentation.

  • Tablet punches: retain upper/lower position, set identity, tooling format, and drawing revision.
  • Tablet compression dies: identify bore and construction requirements alongside the press and mating punch set.
  • Tablet press feed frames: provide the feeder assembly, component location, and available manual reference.
  • Compression rollers and scraper blades: identify the press, roller or blade location, assembly reference, and any wear or damage evidence.

PharmaGlobalEng also maintains a parts catalog. A catalog entry is a starting point; confirm the actual reference against the equipment and application before purchase.

What to include in a parts request

  1. Equipment manufacturer, model, serial or site asset identifier if available.
  2. Part name, number, and where the number came from (component, manual, drawing, or prior invoice).
  3. Assembly location, quantity, orientation, and any mating components.
  4. Drawing number and revision; note discrepancies or machine modifications.
  5. Photographs of the full assembly and part markings, plus close-ups of damage.
  6. Reason for replacement, urgency, and any relevant operating or maintenance history.

For a matched set or partially replaced assembly, identify retained components as well as requested replacements. Confirmed compatibility, availability, lead time, and pricing will be supplied after the request is reviewed.

Why investigate a repeat part failure?

A replacement resolves the immediate parts need; it does not automatically explain why a component failed. If the same part repeatedly wears or breaks, retain the old part, capture the failure location, document the press position and recent maintenance, and compare the observation with other stations. A recurring failure may call for a broader tooling, press, or operating-condition investigation.

Use tooling inspection for condition evidence, tooling failure analysis when the mechanism remains uncertain, and the format compatibility guide to resolve configuration questions before replacement.
