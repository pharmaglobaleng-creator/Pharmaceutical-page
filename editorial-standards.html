Publication transparency

Editorial and Technical Review Standards

These standards explain how PharmaGlobalEng prepares technical material, selects supporting sources, identifies limitations, and handles corrections. Publication on this website does not replace product-specific validation, a manufacturer’s quality system, or applicable regulatory responsibilities.

Source selection

Primary sources first

Regulatory statements should link to the responsible regulator or official legal text. Standards are referenced through the issuing organization when accessible. Research claims should favor original peer-reviewed studies over unsourced summaries.

Traceable citations

A reader should be able to identify the source supporting a factual statement. Sources are paraphrased rather than copied at length, and a citation does not imply that its publisher endorses PharmaGlobalEng.

No invented evidence

Measurements, tolerances, customer outcomes, reviewers, certifications, and technical conclusions are not published unless they can be supported and represented accurately.

Review status and limitations

Source verification and subject-matter review are different checks. A source-linked item may accurately paraphrase a document while still requiring an experienced engineer to assess application context.

Published and source-checked

The cited source has been checked and the published wording is intended to reflect it faithfully. This status does not make the statement universally applicable to every product, machine, material, or process.

Technical review required

Material remains unpublished or clearly restricted when its engineering interpretation, applicability, or supporting evidence requires qualified human review. Review status must not be implied through fabricated names or credentials.

Writing principles

One clear subject per pageNatural engineering languageLimitations stated with recommendationsNo keyword stuffingNo hidden search-engine textNo fabricated structured dataNo unsupported guarantees

Corrections and updates

Technical pages may be revised when official guidance changes, a source becomes unavailable, an ambiguity is identified, or a qualified review changes the interpretation. Material corrections should update the page, its recorded review information where present, and the associated fact inventory.
