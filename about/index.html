Our Story

More Than Five Decades of Pharmaceutical Tooling Experience

Our legacy is not simply how long we have been in business. It is what our family has spent that time learning, improving, and solving.

The PharmaGlobalEng story began with a direct manufacturing challenge. Tablet sticking and picking can reduce tablet quality, interrupt production, damage embossed detail, increase cleaning frequency, and make an otherwise capable tablet press difficult to operate consistently. Our founder recognized that the solution could not be reduced to a single generic answer. Tooling condition, punch-face geometry, surface finish, engraving, coatings, formulation behavior, cleaning, heat, moisture, and compression conditions can all influence release behavior.

That early focus on solving sticking and picking became the foundation of our engineering philosophy: identify the real mechanism, understand the tooling, correct the surface or component condition when appropriate, and match the solution to the application rather than forcing every problem into the same treatment.

Over the decades, that approach expanded into a broader pharmaceutical tooling and component business. Today, PharmaGlobalEng supports customers through tablet tooling restoration, precision polishing, surface engineering, coating evaluation, tooling inspection, failure analysis, lifecycle support, tablet press troubleshooting, replacement parts, and technical problem solving.

Where It Started

The Sticking & Picking Problem That Shaped Our Company

A manufacturing problem with no room for shortcuts.

When powder or granulation adheres to a tablet punch face, the problem may appear as broad sticking or as localized picking in lettering, logos, score lines, islands, or recessed features. The visible defect is only the symptom. The real cause may involve the surface, geometry, coating, material, formulation, press conditions, cleaning process, or a combination of factors.

Our founder’s work centered on developing a practical solution to that problem—an approach that became part of the company’s identity and the beginning of our long-term focus on tooling surfaces and tablet release.

“Do not treat the symptom alone. Understand why the tablet is sticking, then engineer the tooling and surface around the real cause.”

That philosophy still guides our work today.

We do not believe every sticking problem needs the same coating, every worn punch should automatically be replaced, or every performance issue comes from the tooling. The strongest solution starts with evidence.

Working with PharmaGlobalEng

An enquiry does not need to begin with a treatment already selected. Begin with the part, tooling set, or production problem and the decision your team needs to make. For a replacement component, the most useful starting information is the machine model, the existing part reference, its assembly location, and any available drawing. For a tooling concern, include photographs of the whole tool and the affected feature, together with the relevant service history.

The component reference explains how to organize identification details. The services guide distinguishes inspection, evaluation, failure analysis, restoration, and ongoing maintenance. If the request starts with a tablet defect rather than a known tooling problem, the solutions guide helps describe the symptom and select the relevant evidence. These are different routes to the same practical objective: a clear next step based on the actual application.

State what is known and what still needs confirmation. An unreadable part number, an undocumented coating, or a missing drawing should remain an open question rather than becoming an assumed specification. Confirm the scope of work, required records, and any shipping arrangements before sending components. Availability and compatibility for a replacement, and suitability for restoration, depend on the particular enquiry.

Our editorial standards explain how technical information and catalog references are presented. Reference guides help prepare an informed conversation; they do not replace the manufacturer’s own specifications or approval procedures. Use the contact page to provide the project information and arrange the next discussion.

A technical catalog with a defined purpose

The website brings three types of information together: company services, explanations of manufacturing problems, and a catalog for identifying listed replacement components. Each has a different purpose. A service page explains a question that can be brought for review. A solution page helps describe a symptom and organize an investigation. A catalog page provides the available identity and reference information for a particular listed item.

A catalog identifier should remain distinct from an OEM reference. Use the machine identity and actual component information with an enquiry, even when a listing appears to match. Where an image is described as representative, read it as an illustration rather than dimensional evidence. Compatibility, availability, and the scope of supply need confirmation for the particular request. This approach keeps the convenience of a searchable catalog connected to the details needed for a responsible purchasing decision.

The same distinction applies to technical guidance. A general explanation can help a maintenance or production team prepare useful questions, but the actual tooling drawing, machine configuration, product requirements, and inspection findings determine what is appropriate. An answer that depends on missing evidence should remain conditional. The purpose of the resource is to make the next discussion more specific, not to turn an uncertain identification or diagnosis into a confident claim.

Information for the people involved in a tooling decision

A maintenance team may begin with the damaged component and its service history. Production may contribute the timing of a defect and the sequence of operating observations. Purchasing may need a confirmed reference, quantity, and quotation scope. The team responsible for quality may need the applicable acceptance criteria and documentation. Connecting those perspectives avoids losing the technical reason for a request as it moves from an observation to an order.

For a first contact, identify the person coordinating the enquiry and the decision the group needs to make. Explain which records and physical items are available, which information is still being collected, and any timing constraints. That shared description gives the discussion a clear starting point and helps prevent an identification question, a service request, and a production-approval decision from being treated as the same task.

Keep a multi-component enquiry connected

If a project involves several components, prepare one list linking each item to its machine, assembly, reference, quantity, and question. Keep photographs and drawing filenames consistent with that list. Identify superseded documents instead of leaving several revisions unexplained. This gives the people reviewing the request a shared reference and helps distinguish a new requirement from a clarification of an earlier one. When the scope changes, explain which items or decisions are affected so the discussion continues from the current information.
