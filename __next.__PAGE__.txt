  <section class="faq-section" id="faq">
    <h2>Pharmaceutical Tablet Tooling FAQs</h2>
    <p>Technical answers about tablet punch restoration, polishing, sticking, picking, coatings, engraving, surface engineering, inspection, and compression tooling support.</p>
    <div class="faq-list">
      <details><summary>What is pharmaceutical tablet tooling?</summary><p>Pharmaceutical tablet tooling includes punches and dies used in tablet presses to form, identify, and control compressed tablets.</p></details>
<details><summary>What services does PharmaGlobalEng provide?</summary><p>PharmaGlobalEng provides tablet punch restoration, precision polishing, surface engineering, coating evaluation, engraving optimization, tooling inspection, and compression-tooling support.</p></details>
<details><summary>What causes tablet sticking during compression?</summary><p>Tablet sticking may be influenced by formulation moisture, lubrication, tooling surface condition, punch geometry, heat, press speed, and compression settings.</p></details>
<details><summary>What causes tablet picking?</summary><p>Tablet picking is localized adhesion that often occurs in logos, lettering, score lines, or other recessed punch-face features.</p></details>
<details><summary>What is the difference between tablet sticking and picking?</summary><p>Sticking generally affects a broader punch-face area, while picking is localized around engraved or recessed features.</p></details>
<details><summary>How can tablet sticking be reduced?</summary><p>Solutions may include controlled polishing, tooling restoration, engraving refinement, surface engineering, coating evaluation, formulation review, or process adjustments.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can worn tablet punches be restored?</summary><p>Many punches can be restored when wear or damage remains within acceptable dimensional and functional limits.</p></details>
<details><summary>When should tablet punches be replaced?</summary><p>Replacement may be required when critical dimensions, geometry, structural integrity, engraving, or coating condition cannot be safely restored.</p></details>
<details><summary>How does punch polishing improve tablet compression?</summary><p>Controlled polishing can reduce surface irregularities and product-retention sites while supporting cleaner tablet release.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can polishing damage tablet engraving?</summary><p>Improper polishing can soften engraving edges or alter depth, so polishing should preserve critical tooling geometry.</p></details>
<details><summary>What is pharmaceutical surface engineering?</summary><p>It is the controlled optimization of tooling surfaces to influence release, friction, wear, corrosion resistance, cleanability, and consistency.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can coatings help reduce tablet sticking?</summary><p>Certain coatings may help in appropriate applications, but selection should be based on the root cause, formulation, tooling, and operating conditions.</p></details>
<details><summary>What is chromium nitride coating?</summary><p>Chromium nitride, or CrN, is a hard coating used in some tooling applications for wear, corrosion, and surface-performance benefits.</p></details>
<details><summary>What is diamond-like carbon coating?</summary><p>Diamond-like carbon, or DLC, is a family of low-friction, wear-resistant carbon-based coatings used in suitable tooling applications.</p></details>
<details><summary>Which coating is best for sticky formulations?</summary><p>There is no universal best coating; suitability depends on formulation chemistry, moisture, cleaning, tooling material, geometry, and process conditions.</p></details>
<details><summary>How does engraving affect tablet picking?</summary><p>Sharp internal corners, narrow spacing, rough walls, deep recesses, or worn engraving can increase product retention and picking risk.</p></details>
<details><summary>Why do embossed tablets experience picking?</summary><p>Material can remain inside recessed punch features and pull away from the tablet during release.</p></details>
<details><summary>How often should tablet tooling be inspected?</summary><p>Inspection frequency should reflect production volume, formulation abrasiveness, cleaning procedures, coating condition, and tooling history.</p></details>
<details><summary>What signs show that tooling needs attention?</summary><p>Warning signs include buildup, sticking, picking, corrosion, scratches, chipped edges, fading embossing, uneven wear, or poor tablet release.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can damaged coated tooling be restored?</summary><p>Some coated tooling can be restored depending on the coating, damage, substrate condition, remaining dimensions, and recoating requirements.</p></details>
<details><summary>Does every sticky formulation need a coating?</summary><p>No. Some issues are better addressed through polishing, engraving refinement, restoration, moisture control, lubrication, or process changes.</p></details>
<details><summary>Does PharmaGlobalEng serve international manufacturers?</summary><p>Yes. PharmaGlobalEng supports pharmaceutical manufacturers in the United States and international markets.</p></details>
<details><summary>What should be included with a tooling inquiry?</summary><p>Include tooling photos, tablet images, tooling type, defect location, coating details, cleaning method, process conditions, and the desired outcome.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can PharmaGlobalEng evaluate a problem using photographs?</summary><p>Photographs can support a preliminary review, although final recommendations may require physical inspection or additional process information.</p></details>
<details><summary>How do customers request a tooling evaluation?</summary><p>Customers can submit contact details, tooling information, photographs, defect descriptions, and technical objectives for review.</p></details>
