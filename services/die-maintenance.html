Frequently Asked Questions

Pharmaceutical Tablet Die Maintenance FAQ

What is pharmaceutical tablet die maintenance?

It is a pharmaceutical compression or tooling-maintenance topic that should be evaluated using actual process, tooling, formulation, equipment, cleaning, and lifecycle evidence.

What data should be collected?

Collect force, speed, dwell time, feeder settings, temperature, ejection behavior, tooling identity, station history, formulation observations, and defect timing.

Can photographs support a preliminary review?

Yes. Clear photographs support triage, although final recommendations may require physical inspection or additional data.

Why is station mapping useful?

It helps separate localized tooling or machine causes from formulation-wide or process-wide causes.

Should one variable be changed at a time?

Yes. Controlled trials make results easier to interpret and repeat.

How important is tooling condition?

Wear, corrosion, geometry, coatings, alignment, and die-bore condition can affect compression performance.

How important is formulation history?

Moisture, binder, lubricant, granulation, fines, particle size, and hold time may influence the condition.

How important is cleaning?

Cleaning chemistry, abrasives, rinse quality, drying, and residue can create or worsen problems.

Can polishing solve every problem?

No. Polishing may help recoverable surface damage but cannot correct every formulation, press, or geometry issue.

Does every problem require a coating?

No. Coatings are one option and should follow root-cause evaluation.

When is restoration appropriate?

When integrity, dimensions, geometry, and critical features remain acceptable.

When should tooling be replaced?

For cracks, severe dimensional loss, unsafe damage, or unrecoverable geometry.

Does PharmaGlobalEng support international manufacturers?

Yes, subject to scope, shipping, documentation, customs, and service availability.

Who approves return to production?

The manufacturer remains responsible for product, process, quality, validation, and return-to-service approval.

What is the first step?

Define the problem precisely and collect representative, traceable evidence before selecting a corrective action.
