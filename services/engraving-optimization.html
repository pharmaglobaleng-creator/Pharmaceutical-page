Locate the picking at the actual tablet feature

Picking around a logo, letter or score detail gives the tooling review a specific starting point. Match the missing material on the tablet to the corresponding punch feature. Keep the upper and lower faces distinct and orient the photographs consistently. A reversed image can make a defect appear to belong to the wrong part of a letter, particularly where the identification is asymmetric.

Describe the feature before naming a remedy. Is material missing from the middle of a closed letter, along one stroke, between two characters or at the junction with the surrounding cup? Include an acceptable tablet for comparison. A general statement that the logo is poor can conceal several different observations, including incomplete filling, picking and damage after compression.

PharmaGlobalEng helps connect that observation to the tooling condition and a suitable review. A design question, a damaged engraving and a retained deposit require different work. We use the pattern to decide whether inspection, controlled polishing, surface treatment or a design discussion is the useful next step.

Compare the approved drawing with the punch in service

Begin with the drawing revision used to make or approve the tooling. Check the identity of the submitted punch against the set record and establish whether the design has changed since the successful reference run. A current marketing logo file is not a substitute for the tooling drawing: it may show the appearance without the feature dimensions or manufacturing requirements.

Inspect the relevant detail after the approved cleaning procedure, while retaining photographs of the as-found deposit. Distinguish a feature that matches the drawing from one that has become rounded, scratched, chipped or otherwise altered. If the nominal design is suitable but the current punch differs from it, the first decision concerns tool condition rather than a new logo.

Use a feature-by-feature comparison. Ask which observation can be resolved by an image and which needs dimensional or surface inspection. Do not infer the original stroke depth from a worn feature. When the drawing or revision is unavailable, make that gap part of the review scope before requesting work that could permanently change the identification.

Review lettering, counters and closely spaced detail

The enclosed spaces within characters and the narrow regions between strokes deserve individual attention. A character that looks open on a large screen may leave a much tighter feature at tablet size. Tooling design guidance identifies open character spaces and avoidance of sharp trapping corners as useful considerations for reducing picking. These are design considerations, not permission to substitute a font on an approved product.

Compare the actual repeated defect with each suspect feature. A problem concentrated in a closed letter asks a different question from material lost across the complete word. Mark the affected region on a copy of the drawing and retain an unmarked original. If adjacent letters remain satisfactory, they may provide a useful local comparison within the same tooling design.

A proposed change should explain what it changes geometrically and how the required identification remains recognizable. Enlarging a clearance can affect stroke width, character spacing or the available face area. Evaluate those consequences together instead of approving an isolated edit because it makes one small feature easier to access.

Consider stroke profile as well as the plan view

A flat artwork view does not describe the complete surface that contacts the compact. The profile of a letter includes its depth, side geometry and transitions into the surrounding face. Experimental work with model punch features found that changing feature steepness affected detachment behavior differently across tested materials. That supports an application-specific review rather than one universal engraving profile.

For a suspected profile issue, identify the cross-section or measurement that would answer the question. A photograph directly above the punch may show a stroke outline while leaving its depth unresolved. Request a comparison with the relevant drawing features and state any limits of the inspection method. Keep uncertainty about inaccessible regions visible rather than assigning dimensions from appearance alone.

The engineering decision should preserve the intended tablet identification and the tool's required geometry. A local blend, reduced feature depth or revised profile is a design change when it departs from the approved specification. It should have an agreed drawing basis and an evaluation plan before manufacture or modification is arranged.

Review cup depth together with the engraving layout

Tablet tooling cup depth and sticking should be considered in the context of the complete tablet profile. The curved face provides the surface on which the identification must fit; changing the cup can change the room available for lettering and the surrounding transitions. Manufacturer guidance notes that deep profiles can constrain the embossing area. Cup depth is therefore a relevant design variable, but it does not identify the cause of every deposit.

Review the position of the troublesome character relative to the face center, perimeter and any changing curvature. Compare the affected upper and lower tooling where their profiles differ. Keep a face-wide sticking complaint separate from localized picking at an individual feature. The same tablet may exhibit both, and each should retain its own observation record.

A shallower cup is not an automatic correction. The approved tablet shape, dimensions, identification and other product requirements must remain part of any redesign. We can discuss the tooling question and relevant service route, while the manufacturer's responsible teams determine whether a proposed geometry change is acceptable for the product.

Decide whether the job concerns the surface or the design

If the feature matches its drawing but carries recoverable surface damage, controlled polishing may be the appropriate service to assess. The proposed work needs to identify the affected region and the features that must remain intact. Polishing that erases a sharp boundary or rounds a required detail can change the tool even when the finished surface looks brighter.

A coating enquiry addresses another part of the problem. It asks whether a defined surface treatment is suitable for the existing material, geometry and release complaint. It does not establish that an unsuitable engraving layout has been corrected. Coating history also matters when surface work is considered on a tool that already carries a treatment.

Keep the alternatives explicit in the quotation discussion: inspect the current detail, assess a recoverable surface, evaluate a coating, or develop a drawing-based design proposal. Some findings may support more than one route. The recommendation should explain why a route fits the observed feature and what evidence would change that recommendation.

Prepare a useful engraving and logo review package

Send an overview of the tablet and punch followed by close views of the affected detail. Label the faces, tooling identity and drawing revision. State whether the problem follows one character across a complete set, appears on selected punches or emerged after a maintenance operation. Include the first observed point in the run and the interval before the feature becomes affected again after cleaning.

Keep the evidence manageable. A short annotated comparison between a satisfactory and an affected feature is more useful for the first discussion than a folder of unlabeled photographs. Include coating and prior polishing information when available. If a proposed design already exists, show the current and proposed revisions separately so the requested change can be understood.

Tell us the decision you need: whether the current engraving should be inspected, whether surface work is potentially suitable or whether the design deserves further review. Contact PharmaGlobalEng before sending tooling. We can discuss the service scope and the information needed to determine the next step.

Evaluate legibility and recurrence after the proposed work

Judge the result against the original complaint and the approved identification requirements. Retain comparable views of the relevant character before and after the work. Check whether the modified or restored feature meets its specification, then evaluate the corresponding tablet under the manufacturer's agreed production and quality procedures.

The observation period matters. A clean letter at startup does not answer whether picking returns at the point when the previous run became affected. Record the interval to recurrence, the number of affected samples and any cleaning interruptions. Keep a legibility finding separate from a conclusion about every other product-quality requirement.

If both the engraving and coating changed, record the result as a combined intervention. Improvement can support a practical decision without showing which individual change caused it. Retain unsuccessful results as well. A documented comparison gives future maintenance and design discussions a stronger basis than a claim that one logo style always prevents picking.

Questions about tablet tooling design for picking

Can you change a tablet logo without a drawing review?

A change that alters the approved identification or tooling geometry needs an agreed design basis. We first establish the existing drawing and what the proposed work would change.

Does deeper engraving always make a clearer tablet?

Depth is only one feature of the profile. Legibility, the surrounding geometry and release behavior need to be considered together for the actual tablet and tooling.

Should picking around a letter always receive a coating?

The feature may need cleaning assessment, inspection, surface work or design review. Coating is one possible route when the tooling condition and application support it.
