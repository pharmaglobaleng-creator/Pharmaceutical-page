Condition evidence for maintenance decisions

Tablet Tooling Inspection

A structured inspection establishes the observable condition of tablet punches and dies before restoration, reuse, escalation, or replacement decisions are made. PharmaGlobalEng reviews submitted tooling against an agreed scope and reports findings without treating inspection alone as proof of process capability.

Inspection scope

What can be examined

Scope is selected for the tooling set, its history, and the decision the inspection needs to support.

Working surfaces

Punch tips, cups, lands, engraving, die bores, and other product-contact areas are reviewed for adhesion, scoring, corrosion, edge damage, and finish changes.

Functional features

Heads, necks, barrels, keyways, tips, and dies can be checked for visible wear, damage, and dimensional observations relevant to the agreed tooling specification.

Set-level patterns

Repeated or localized findings are compared across the submitted set to help distinguish isolated damage from broader maintenance or operating patterns.

Inspection workflow

From intake to engineering review

Define the question and acceptance basisConfirm tooling identity, available drawings or specifications, service history, reported symptoms, sampling plan, and requested measurements.
Receive and document conditionRecord the submitted quantity, identifiers, packaging condition, and relevant chain-of-custody information supplied with the tooling.
Clean as agreed and inspectUse suitable visual, magnified, surface, and dimensional checks within the approved scope. Cleaning method and any limitations are recorded.
Compare findingsReview observations against available specifications, set patterns, known service conditions, and the limits of the inspection method.
Report and review next stepsProvide traceable findings, disposition categories where defined, and recommendations for maintenance, deeper analysis, monitoring, or replacement review.

Evidence collected

Documented observations

  • Tool and set identifiers
  • Photographs of relevant findings
  • Wear and damage locations
  • Selected dimensional results
  • Surface-condition observations
  • Exceptions and inspection constraints

Engineering outputs

Decision-ready reporting

  • Inspection summary by tool or sample
  • Finding classifications
  • Comparison with supplied criteria
  • Potential maintenance actions
  • Items requiring further analysis
  • Recommendations for monitoring or review

Technical limitations

What inspection does not establish by itself

A tooling inspection describes condition within the submitted sample, agreed methods, and available criteria. It does not by itself validate a manufacturing process, establish product quality, certify tooling for use, identify every subsurface defect, or prove a single root cause for a compression problem. Material testing, press data, formulation information, drawings, or failure analysis may be needed for a broader conclusion.
