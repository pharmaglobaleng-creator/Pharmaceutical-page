KORSCH announced its XT 300 tablet press in May 2026, highlighting access to the compression zone, cleaning and maintenance access, and an exchangeable turret system. Those features give tablet-manufacturing teams useful topics to examine when evaluating press access and tooling changeovers. Product details and performance figures below are reported by KORSCH and have not been independently verified by PharmaGlobalEng.

## What did KORSCH announce about the XT 300?

In its [May 2026 announcement](https://www.korsch.com/blog/korsch-unveils-xt-300-tablet-press-at-interpack/), KORSCH describes the XT 300 as a single-sided rotary press for single-layer tablet production. The company says the press can produce up to 324,000 tablets per hour when equipped with a segmented turret. That is the manufacturer's stated maximum, not a guarantee of output under a particular product, tooling setup, or operating condition.

KORSCH also describes large inspection windows, access to the compression zone, a swiveling upper compression station, and an exchangeable turret system. The announcement says these design features are intended to support visibility, cleaning, maintenance, and turret exchange. Their practical effect will depend on the facility's equipment configuration, procedures, and production needs.

## What should a team check when evaluating tooling access?

Press access matters because inspection and maintenance tasks must be carried out on the actual equipment under the site's procedures. During an equipment review, teams can ask for a demonstration of the relevant access points and document what must be removed, moved, or isolated to inspect or service the tooling area.

- Which turret and die configurations are available for the intended product formats?
- What tooling identification, drawings, and format documentation are supplied for each configuration?
- Which steps are required for turret exchange, cleaning, and tooling inspection?
- What clearances, tools, and handling steps are needed to reach the components being serviced?
- How are inspection findings and replaced components recorded and traced?

These questions are an evaluation checklist, not a conclusion that one press design is better or that a particular tooling set is compatible. Confirm configuration and compatibility details with the equipment manufacturer and the site's qualified engineering team.

## How can teams compare changeovers usefully?

Compare the same defined task across candidate configurations. Record the starting condition, the steps performed, personnel involved, tools required, time observed, and any safety or quality controls. Separate manufacturer specifications from measurements made during a site demonstration. This creates a more useful comparison than relying on headline throughput alone.
