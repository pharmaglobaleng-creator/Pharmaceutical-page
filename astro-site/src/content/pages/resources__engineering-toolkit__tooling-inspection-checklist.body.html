01 · Purpose and boundaries

What is a tablet compression tooling inspection worksheet?

A tablet compression tooling inspection worksheet is a record for documenting observed punch and die conditions, measurements, supporting evidence, and site review. Use it to organize an investigation; it does not decide whether tooling is acceptable or safe to return to production.

Use this framework to organize observations when tooling shows sticking, picking, wear, surface damage, corrosion, deformation, or suspected dimensional change. Connect each symptom to tool identity, press position, process context, inspection evidence, and documented follow-up.

It does not determine whether a punch or die is acceptable for use. Tool acceptance criteria, measurement methods, repair limits, coating selection, and return-to-service authorization must come from applicable drawings, supplier specifications, equipment instructions, and the site’s approved quality system.

Risk scoring is site-defined. ICH Q9(R1) describes quality risk management principles and example methods, including FMEA. It does not prescribe a universal Severity × Probability × Detectability scale or a universal action threshold for tablet tooling. Define and approve any scoring model locally before use.

02 · Scope

System and items to identify

  • Tablet press, model, turret/station, tooling set ID, and controlled drawing revision
  • Configuration: single-tip or multi-tip; tips per punch; site/vendor tip numbering and orientation convention
  • For a multi-tip observation, record affected punch (upper/lower), station, individual tip position, orientation, and matching die ID
  • Punch and die identifiers, serial/lot numbers, and drawing revision
  • Tool steel, surface treatment or coating, if documented
  • Product/formulation and batch context, handled under site confidentiality controls
  • Observed defect, date/time, operator report, and inspection method
03 · Minimum record

Capture evidence before disposition

  • Photographs with scale and orientation where practical
  • Measured values, units, instrument ID, calibration status, and method
  • Tool history, cleaning/maintenance history, and prior repairs
  • Relevant process records and tablet defect observations
  • Reviewer, date, decision rationale, actions, and closure evidence

Open a structured evidence checklist
  • Identify if the observation is isolated to one station/tool or appears across multiple stations.
  • Record whether the issue is new, recurring, increasing, or associated with a process/material change.
  • Use approved cleaning and inspection methods; preserve samples or images according to site procedures.
  • Separate measured facts from hypotheses. Label each hypothesis and list what evidence would confirm or rule it out.
  • Route potential product-quality impact through the site’s deviation, investigation, and change-control processes.

09 · Glossary

Terms used in tooling observations

Sticking
Tablet material adheres to a punch face or other tooling surface during compression/ejection.
Picking
Localized sticking in engraved, embossed, or recessed punch features, which may pull material from the tablet surface.
Capping
Separation of a tablet’s crown or cap from the body, often observed during ejection or handling; causes can be multifactorial.
Lamination
Separation of a tablet into two or more layers or horizontal planes.
Filming
A thin residue layer or film accumulating on a tooling surface; teams may use the term differently, so document the observed condition.
Tip land
The narrow peripheral region at the punch tip surrounding the cup/profile, as defined by the tool drawing.
Barrel
The punch body section guided within the press turret/bushing, with boundaries and features defined by tooling design.
Head
The punch end engaged by press cams/rollers; terminology and geometry can vary by punch design.
Cup depth
Depth of the concave punch-tip cup measured from a defined reference plane to a specified point/feature under the applicable drawing method.
Land wear
Wear, rounding, deformation, or dimensional change observed at the tip land; assessment requires comparison with controlled design requirements.
Chrome flaking
Visible separation or loss of a chromium-containing coating/deposit. Record location, extent, images, and substrate exposure; do not infer product impact or disposition from appearance alone.

12 · Engineering and quality disposition

Use controlled criteria for repair, coating, or retirement

Inspection or measurement

Compare measured condition with the current approved drawing/specification and the tool supplier’s instructions. Record instrument, method, units, calibration status, and uncertainty where relevant.

Polishing or restoration

Confirm authorized repair limits and the post-repair inspection plan with the responsible engineering and quality functions. Do not infer that polishing is acceptable from a visual defect alone.

Coating or surface modification

Review the exact coating supplier, process, substrate compatibility, dimensional impact, cleanliness, documentation, and intended product-contact use. Use the current supplier technical data and site qualification/change-control process.

Retirement or replacement

Use controlled acceptance/rejection criteria and document tool identity, reason, impact assessment, and replacement/verification record under site procedures.

No universal coating-property values are shown here. Hardness, friction, temperature capability, thickness, and release behavior vary by coating formulation, deposition process, test method, substrate, and service conditions. Obtain values from the supplier’s applicable datasheet and evaluate them for the intended application.

13 · Suggested documentation flow

Keep observations, analysis, and decisions traceable

  1. Describe the observation. Record the symptom, affected station/tool, date, batch context, and supporting images or measurements.
  2. Establish the evidence. Check the relevant controlled drawing, tool history, inspection records, cleaning/maintenance records, and process information.
  3. Assess quality risk. Use the site-approved method and involve the functions required by local procedures. Note uncertainties and alternative causes.
  4. Approve a controlled action. Engineering and Quality determine inspection, repair, coating, replacement, containment, or further investigation under the site quality system.
  5. Verify and close. Record post-action results, return-to-service authorization, change/deviation references, and evidence that actions were completed.
