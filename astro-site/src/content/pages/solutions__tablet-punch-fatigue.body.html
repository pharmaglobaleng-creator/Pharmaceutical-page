Tablet Punch Fatigue Failure

Tablet punch fatigue failure involves damage accumulating under repeated loading. PharmaGlobalEng helps investigate the affected tooling and its service history, including crack location, surface condition and loading evidence. A suspected crack requires assessment; surface polishing is not a repair for lost structural integrity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Tablet Punch Fatigue Failure FAQ

What is tablet punch fatigue failure?

It is a pharmaceutical tooling topic that should be evaluated using the actual tooling condition, process history, material, geometry, coating, cleaning, handling, and risk.

What evidence should be collected?

Collect clear photographs, tooling identity, station history, dimensions, material and coating records, production volume, cleaning methods, and prior service history.

Can photographs support a preliminary review?

Yes. Photographs can support triage, but final disposition may require physical inspection or additional testing.

Can polishing repair cracks?

No. Cracks are structural defects and should not be treated as a cosmetic polishing problem.

Can polishing improve recoverable surface damage?

Yes, when scratches, light corrosion, or roughness are recoverable and critical dimensions and geometry can be preserved.

How are restore-versus-replace decisions made?

The decision should compare integrity, dimensions, geometry, damage mechanism, remaining allowance, risk, and service requirements.

How often should tooling be inspected?

Inspection frequency should reflect production volume, formulation abrasiveness, corrosion exposure, coating history, prior failures, and criticality.

Does every worn tool require replacement?

No. Some tools may be restorable when damage is recoverable and critical requirements remain acceptable.

Does every problem require a coating?

No. Coatings are one possible intervention and should follow root-cause evaluation.

How important is cleaning?

Cleaning chemistry, abrasives, rinse quality, drying, and handling can create or accelerate damage.

How important is traceability?

Set and station identity are essential for linking defects, service history, measurements, and corrective actions.

Can international manufacturers request an evaluation?

Yes, subject to technical scope, shipping, documentation, customs, and service availability.

Who approves return to production?

The manufacturer remains responsible for product, process, quality, validation, and return-to-service approval.

What should an inspection report include?

Identification, photographs, measurements, observations, work performed, limitations, and final disposition.

What is the first step?

Define the observed condition precisely and collect representative, traceable evidence before selecting an action.
