Define the deposit before naming its cause

Record whether material forms a broad layer, a localized patch or a deposit around an engraved feature. Distinguish loose dust from material that remains attached after the approved handling or cleaning step. Keep the same photograph orientation and magnification when comparing tools. Describe what is visible before attributing it to surface energy, electrostatic effects or moisture.

This page addresses how to investigate the powder–surface interaction. Use the sticking and picking guides when the task is to classify the resulting tablet defect. A deposit can be evidence in that investigation, but its presence does not automatically explain every mark on the tablet.

Compare powder and surface histories together

Identify the formulation or development sample, batch and available material-condition records. Note relevant changes to preparation, handling or storage. On the tooling side, record the steel, coating identity if known, service history and cleaning method. Without both histories, an apparent coating comparison may actually compare different powders or differently maintained tools.

Where only one tool is affected, compare its local features with an unaffected counterpart from the same run. Where an entire set behaves differently from an earlier run, review shared conditions before concluding that every tool has the same new defect. Keep unknown information visible; a guessed coating identity can send the evaluation in the wrong direction.

Distinguish roughness from surface chemistry

Surface texture and surface chemistry describe different aspects of the contact. A roughness value summarizes a measurement made over a defined region and method; it is not a complete description of an engraved cup. A coating name also leaves questions about preparation, final condition and the actual working feature.

Ask where material first accumulates and whether that location was included in the inspection. A clean measurement from a flat area may not characterize the recess where a deposit starts. Avoid a universal “smoother is always better” specification. Define the intended surface condition and evaluate it with the relevant application.

Test a hypothesis with an interpretable comparison

A useful trial starts with a question such as whether the observed deposit recurs after a documented surface treatment under comparable conditions. Define a baseline, sampling points and a consistent way of recording buildup. Capture the time or production exposure to the first relevant observation instead of relying only on an end-of-run photograph.

Keep material and operating changes controlled by the responsible team. Record interrupted or unsuccessful runs. If the intervention requires several simultaneous changes, acknowledge that the result evaluates the combined package, not a single mechanism. Laboratory measurements can help develop a hypothesis; they do not replace a representative manufacturing evaluation.

Decide whether to clean, restore or change the surface

An approved cleaning step may establish that the visible feature is removable product. It does not explain why the deposit returns. A damaged cup or engraving may require a geometry review before surface treatment. A coating comparison should specify the substrate and finished feature, the observation being addressed and the acceptance criteria.

Send PharmaGlobalEng the tool identity, deposit map, paired tool/tablet photographs, surface history and trial observations. State whether you need an evaluation of existing tooling or a proposed surface-treatment scope. The purpose is to establish a useful next decision; no universal coating or polishing instruction can be selected from an unlabeled photograph.

Questions and answers

Does powder adhesion prove a surface-energy problem?

No. The deposit is an observation. A mechanism needs supporting evidence and a comparison designed to test it.

Is adhesion the same as picking?

Picking describes a localized tablet defect, often associated with a feature such as lettering. Adhesion describes material attaching to a surface.

Will a mirror finish prevent all buildup?

No universal finish prevents adhesion for every powder and geometry. Evaluate the specified surface in the relevant application.

Can I compare two coatings by color?

No. Color does not establish the coating specification, preparation, thickness or condition.

Why record time to the first deposit?

It helps distinguish immediate behavior from a condition that develops during the run and makes comparisons more interpretable.

What if cleaning removes all visible material?

Record that result and examine the cleaned surface. If the deposit returns, investigate the recurring interaction rather than assuming cleaning addressed its cause.
