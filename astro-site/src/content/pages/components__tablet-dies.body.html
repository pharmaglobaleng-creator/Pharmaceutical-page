Establish which die is required

Record the die marking, tooling set, machine model and drawing revision. Identify any orientation or assembly features shown in the specification. Keep the associated punch references with the record. Two dies producing similar tablet outlines can still differ in dimensions, materials or installation requirements.

If only a used sample is available, label which information is verified and which is inferred. A worn feature is not a reliable manufacturing specification. Provide photographs of all accessible faces and any markings, with measured features clearly identified. Confirm the required specification before ordering a replacement.

Inspect the bore at the relevant locations

Examine the entry, working region and ejection path for deposits, scoring and apparent dimensional change. Record where each finding occurs rather than describing the whole bore as “rough.” Inspect after approved cleaning while retaining useful as-found photographs. A deposit can conceal damage or mimic a surface change.

Measurements need an identified location and method. A single diameter reading does not characterize variation through a bore, and a photograph cannot establish a dimensional tolerance. Use the approved drawing to define the features and acceptance limits. For shaped bores or difficult-to-access features, confirm that the selected measurement method can assess the intended requirement.

Connect tablet observations with tooling evidence

Where the complaint involves sidewall marks or difficult ejection, retain labeled tablets and available operating records. Compare the surface location on the tablet with the die observation. Establish whether the symptom follows one station, several tools or a change in product or maintenance history.

The correlation is a starting point, not proof that the die caused the defect. Mating punches, product behavior and machine conditions may require review. Use the binding guide for the broader ejection investigation and keep a die replacement decision tied to verified component condition.

Evaluate maintenance without changing the specification by accident

Do not assume that polishing a bore is a neutral cleaning step. Material removal can change a controlled feature. A proposed maintenance scope should identify what will be done, what dimensions will be inspected and whether the tool remains within the applicable requirements afterward.

If a die is cracked, distorted or outside its accepted geometry, route it for a formal disposition. A coating request does not remove the need to establish its underlying condition. Where a maintenance provider proposes restoration, ask for a defined feasibility decision before authorizing work that changes the bore.

Prepare the replacement and return-to-service record

A replacement enquiry should include drawing or confirmed reference, machine identity, quantity, material requirements where specified and the reason for replacement. Distinguish the price and availability question from the compatibility question. A listed catalog item should still be matched to the actual assembly.

After replacement or approved maintenance, retain the incoming inspection, installation record and appropriate production checks. Follow the machine instructions for installation and setup rather than applying a generic clearance or torque. If the original ejection symptom remains, reopen the system investigation instead of repeatedly replacing an unproven component.

Questions and answers

Can I identify a die from tablet diameter alone?

No. The tablet outline does not establish every die dimension, material requirement or machine interface.

Where should die-bore measurements be taken?

Use the drawing and observed damage to define locations and method. An unlabeled reading cannot characterize the full bore.

Do sidewall marks prove that a die is worn?

No. Compare the marks with bore condition and other ejection evidence before assigning the cause.

Is bore polishing always safe maintenance?

No. It can change geometry. Use a defined procedure and verify the relevant dimensions afterward.

Should dies and punches be tracked together?

Preserve their set and mating-tool references so inspection findings can be connected with the actual production configuration.

What information is needed when the drawing is missing?

Send machine and tool markings, photographs and clearly labeled measurements. State that the drawing is unavailable so compatibility remains an explicit question.
