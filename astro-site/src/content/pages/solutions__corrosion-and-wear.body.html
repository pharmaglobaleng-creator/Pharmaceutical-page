What this comparison helps you decide

This guide is for a tooling set showing more than one kind of surface change, or an investigation in which the cause remains uncertain. Its purpose is to route evidence to the right next step. Use the dedicated corrosion or wear guide once the primary question has been established. A coating request should follow that distinction: protecting against an abrasive formulation is a different specification from addressing repeated chemical exposure during cleaning or storage.

Keep four observations separate: material deposited on the surface, apparent loss of base material, damage to an existing coating, and a change in the tool’s dimensions. More than one can be present. Record uncertainty explicitly instead of assigning every dark region to corrosion or every bright region to wear.

Read the location and sequence of damage

A recurring band inside a die bore raises a different question from marks across a punch cup or discoloration in a storage tray. Map the feature on an oriented photograph and identify the individual tool. Note when the condition was first observed: during production, after washing, after drying, or after storage. The timing does not prove the cause, but it determines which records should be compared.

Look for an unaffected comparison from the same set or a documented earlier inspection. If all tools shared a cleaning cycle but only one station is damaged, examine local conditions as well as the common exposure. If previously acceptable tools change after storage, preserve the cleaning, drying, packaging and storage records rather than focusing only on compression settings.

Inspect without removing the evidence

Photograph the as-found condition when site procedures permit, then use the approved cleaning method before dimensional assessment. Identify which marks disappear and which remain. Do not start by polishing away an unexplained defect: that can remove evidence while changing a controlled surface. Suspected cracks, loose coating or missing metal need isolation and assessment under the site’s tooling procedure.

Measure the relevant feature against the approved drawing with a suitable instrument and method. Record units, measurement locations and tool identity. A single roughness reading or overall length does not describe an entire cup, bore or engraved detail. Ask a qualified assessor whether further examination is needed when the mechanism or remaining integrity is unclear.

Separate corrective actions for mixed damage

If chemical exposure is suspected, review material identity, product contact, cleaning compatibility and storage conditions. If progressive mechanical loss is suspected, review the contact region, product abrasiveness, alignment and service history. These are investigation paths, not conclusions from appearance. A mixed finding may need both a maintenance change and a different tooling specification.

Judge cleaning, restoration, coating and replacement against the remaining geometry and the identified exposure. Cleaning removes a deposit; it does not restore missing metal. Polishing changes the surface and may alter a critical feature. Coating adds a layer to a suitable substrate but does not establish the structural condition of that substrate. Replacement can restore the specified component while leaving the original damaging condition unresolved.

Prepare a useful evaluation request

Send the tool drawing or reference, steel and coating information where known, service and cleaning history, dated photographs, inspection results, and a description of the affected tablet or production symptom. Include an unaffected comparison if available. Tell PharmaGlobalEng whether the immediate decision concerns continued use, restoration, surface treatment or replacement so the proposed scope can be confirmed.

For the next inspection, agree what will be compared and what acceptance criteria apply. Retain the earlier images and measurements. Evidence that a surface looks better immediately after service is different from evidence that the damaging mechanism has been controlled over a defined operating period.

Questions and answers

Can corrosion and wear happen together?

Yes. Keep evidence of chemical exposure separate from mechanical contact and dimensional loss, then assess how the findings interact.

Does rust-colored staining prove the tool has corroded?

No. Deposits and contamination can change appearance. Assess the cleaned surface and relevant exposure history before assigning a cause.

Can a coating repair a worn dimension?

A coating should not be assumed to restore an out-of-specification feature. Assess the remaining geometry and an explicitly approved repair scope first.

Should I polish a suspicious pit before inspection?

Preserve the evidence and follow the approved inspection route. Uncontrolled polishing can obscure the condition and change the tool.

Which measurements are needed?

Use the drawing and damage location to define measurements. Record the method, reference surfaces, units and individual tool identity.

When should a damaged tool be replaced?

Replacement depends on integrity, dimensions and an approved disposition. Surface appearance alone is not a return-to-service criterion.
