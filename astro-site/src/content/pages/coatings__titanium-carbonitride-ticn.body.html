Establish whether wear is the limiting condition

Describe the feature that changes during service: a cup region, edge, engraving or another working surface. Separate confirmed material loss from deposits, scratches and a change in appearance. Compare inspection records at the same feature over time when available. Without a baseline, it can be difficult to distinguish a manufacturing feature from a service-related change.

If the principal complaint is product adhering to the punch, treat release as a separate evaluation requirement. A wear-oriented proposal needs to state what it is expected to preserve. It should not be presented as a universal sticking solution simply because the coating is hard.

Read supplier data in its application context

Oerlikon’s coating portfolio includes a TiCN product with its own architecture and published properties. Those values characterize that product and its stated context; they are not acceptance limits for all pharmaceutical tooling. Ask the proposed provider which specification applies to the actual treatment.

Hardness, deposition conditions and other reported properties need their test method and context. A comparison assembled from unrelated brochures can place unlike measurements side by side. Use supplier data to establish a candidate process, then evaluate the complete tool and its intended duty.

Confirm substrate support and local feature condition

The layer and underlying tool work as a system. Provide material identity, heat-treatment history where known and inspection results. A coating order cannot substitute for resolving a damaged edge or uncertain remaining geometry. Ask how the proposed preparation will preserve critical features and how their condition will be checked afterward.

Pay attention to the region where the wear complaint begins. An acceptable measurement on an accessible flat may not answer a question about a small edge or engraved recess. Mark the feature on the drawing and agree what can actually be inspected before and after treatment.

Compare TiCN and TiN without assuming a winner

TiCN and TiN are different material systems. A supplier may offer them for different purposes or with different process details. Select between candidates using the substrate, observed failure, finished dimensions and the provider’s application assessment, rather than treating the names as successive quality grades.

If changing from an established surface system, document the baseline and the reason for the change. Include maintenance and cleaning requirements in the review. A longer interval between visible wear findings would be useful, but it does not compensate for a new tablet defect or an unacceptable change to a controlled feature.

Plan inspection and follow-up before ordering

Define the intended coverage, any excluded regions and the required finished-tool condition. Agree how existing layers will be handled for previously coated tools. Ask what records will identify the delivered coating system and which inspections support acceptance. These details are especially useful when comparing performance across more than one tooling set.

For an application trial, retain matched measurements and photographs at defined intervals and record the operating exposure. Compare tablet quality and maintenance observations as well as tool appearance. Send PharmaGlobalEng the wear record, drawing and material history for a scope discussion. TiCN availability, feasibility and any proposed work must be confirmed for the particular tool.

Questions and answers

What is TiCN?

TiCN is titanium carbonitride, a coating material used in engineered surface systems. The supplier specification defines the actual treatment.

Is TiCN always better than TiN?

No. Suitability depends on the complete coating process, substrate, geometry and application requirements.

Can a high hardness value predict tool life?

Not by itself. The relevant wear mechanism, substrate and operating conditions also need assessment.

Will TiCN rebuild a rounded punch edge?

Do not assume it will restore lost geometry. Inspect the feature and resolve restoration or replacement before treatment.

What should a TiCN trial measure?

Track the specific wear feature and operating exposure, alongside required tablet quality and maintenance outcomes.

Why identify the previous coating before rework?

The existing system influences removal, preparation and inspection requirements. An unknown layer leaves those decisions unresolved.
