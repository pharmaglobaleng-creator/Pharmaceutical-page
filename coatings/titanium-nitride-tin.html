Titanium Nitride (TiN) Coating: Questions for Tablet Tooling Selection

Updated September 20, 2026

Titanium nitride, abbreviated TiN, is a coating material used in engineered surface systems. Specifying TiN for a tablet tool requires more than naming the material: confirm the substrate, coating process, intended working surfaces and finished-tool requirements. This is a selection reference; availability and the proposed treatment scope require confirmation.

Distinguish TiN from a complete coating specification

A material abbreviation does not identify every property of the supplied layer. The coating provider’s actual product and process specification should define the relevant characteristics. Oerlikon lists TiN within its coating portfolio, but data for a named supplier product should not be transferred to every coating sold under the same material description.

For a tooling enquiry, state whether the priority is wear, release behavior, maintenance or a combination. A general hard-coating description does not establish performance against the specific powder, geometry and cleaning exposure. Separate the property you want from the evidence needed to show it in the application.

Assess the steel before specifying the layer

Provide the tool material, heat-treatment information where available, drawing and service history. The provider needs to establish whether the proposed preparation and deposition process are compatible with the tool. Do not confuse a published service-temperature figure with the temperature used during deposition; they answer different questions.

Inspect previously used tooling before treatment. Cracks, dimensional loss and damaged features remain engineering questions even when the order is described as recoating. Ask which incoming findings would prevent treatment and which measurements will be retained as the pre-treatment baseline.

Define coverage and finished geometry

Mark the surfaces that require treatment and any areas that must remain untreated. A punch cup, engraved feature, tip land and machine-contact region can have different requirements. Avoid an undefined all-over instruction when only a particular working region is intended to change.

Preparation and added coating thickness both belong in the finished-dimension review. Ask how the proposed process affects small features and critical interfaces. Keep any design alteration separate from the coating order; a surface-treatment request should not silently authorize changes to the tablet identifier or tooling geometry.

Compare TiN with alternatives using the actual complaint

TiN, TiCN, CrN and DLC are different material descriptions, not a universal ranking of suitability. Begin with the observed limitation and the intended substrate. A coating selected for a wear question may not answer a release question, and a successful result on one product does not establish the result for another.

Use the coating provider’s current data to define a feasible candidate, then agree the application comparison. Specify what will be held constant, what surface condition will be measured, and how the tablet or maintenance outcome will be recorded. The purpose is to choose a defensible treatment, not the most attractive brochure value.

Inspect existing TiN and define a repeatable outcome

Changes in color, exposed areas and visible deposits should be documented separately. Color alone cannot certify composition, thickness or remaining condition. Preserve the as-found observation and compare the same region after approved cleaning. Where the concern is possible coating loss, ask what additional examination is needed.

For recoating, confirm whether removal of the previous layer is necessary and how the remaining tool will be assessed afterward. For new treatment, agree the delivered documentation and acceptance checks. Send PharmaGlobalEng the drawing, substrate, known coating history and intended application so feasibility and scope can be discussed without an unsupported life-extension or release guarantee.

Questions and answers

What does TiN stand for?

TiN stands for titanium nitride. It identifies a material, not every detail of the supplied coating system.

Is a gold-colored tool definitely TiN-coated?

No. Appearance is not sufficient identification. Use the coating specification or an appropriate material assessment.

Does TiN automatically prevent tablet sticking?

No. Release behavior depends on the application and finished surface; it needs relevant evidence.

Can TiN be specified without knowing the tool steel?

The substrate and its condition must be confirmed to assess process compatibility and the finished-tool requirements.

Is coating thickness irrelevant on a punch?

No. Added thickness and preparation can matter to controlled geometry and small features.

What is needed before TiN recoating?

Confirm the existing layer, tool condition, removal requirements and post-treatment acceptance checks before agreeing the scope.
