Tablet Punch Galling

Definition: Tablet punch galling is adhesive wear at a sliding metal contact: local junctions form and tear, transferring or smearing metal between contacting surfaces. It differs from sticking, where formulation adheres to the punch face.

Three contributing mechanisms:

  1. Insufficient or unsuitable lubrication at the affected mechanical contact.
  2. Misalignment, binding or inadequate clearance that concentrates contact load.
  3. An unsuitable contact-material or surface combination, sometimes aggravated by a damaged protective layer.

Immediate investigation checklist:

  1. Identify whether the deposit is metal transfer or formulation; record the station and contact location.
  2. Isolate the affected tooling under the site procedure; check guides, alignment, clearances and lubrication delivery against OEM requirements.
  3. Have the tooling supplier assess scoring and dimensions before any polishing or return to service; investigate the mating component too.

Page outline extension: Add an annotated drawing of punch body/guide and punch/die contacts; include metal transfer versus product adhesion photographs from actual inspections. Link to the existing sticking guide for the alternative diagnosis.

Technical references: I Holland: technical reference; I Holland: steel selection

Tablet Lamination vs. Capping

Definition: Tablet capping is separation of the upper or lower crown from the tablet body. Lamination is separation into two or more layers through the tablet body; both describe a fracture pattern, not a single root cause.

Three contributing mechanisms:

  1. Air retention or an unsuitable compression/decompression profile.
  2. Insufficient bonding or excessive elastic recovery for the formulation and operating speed.
  3. Tooling or ejection-related stress, including worn tip edges or die damage.

Immediate investigation checklist:

  1. Record when fracture appears: ejection, handling or later testing; photograph the fracture plane and record the station.
  2. Review precompression, speed/dwell and main-compression traces against the qualified process; vary one factor only in an authorized trial.
  3. Inspect punch edges and dies, then compare friability, tensile strength and ejection behavior with an acceptable reference batch.

Page outline extension: Include a side-by-side fracture diagram, a timing-of-failure matrix and links to the existing defect overview. Do not claim that increasing force or dwell always cures capping.

Technical references: Natoli: preventing capping; Natoli: punch head inspection

Tool Steel Selection & Substrate Wear

Definition: Tool steel selection matches the underlying punch or die material to the application’s wear, strength, toughness and corrosion requirements. Substrate wear is damage to that base material, whether the tool is uncoated or has lost protection from its coating.

Three contributing mechanisms:

  1. Abrasive formulation particles acting on an inadequately wear-resistant substrate.
  2. Corrosive product, cleaning residues or storage conditions attacking exposed steel.
  3. Fatigue or overload at stress concentrations, exacerbated by material/heat-treatment mismatch.

Immediate investigation checklist:

  1. Classify the damage as abrasion, adhesion, corrosion, deformation or fracture; map its location before selecting another steel.
  2. Check drawing, material certificate, heat treatment and coating history against the application and cleaning chemistry.
  3. Ask the tooling supplier to balance toughness, wear and corrosion resistance; inspect dimensions before deciding on restoration or replacement.

Page outline extension: Add a failure-mode selection matrix rather than a universal best-steel ranking. Separate material selection from claims that PharmaGlobalEng stocks or supplies a specific grade.

Technical references: I Holland: steel selection; I Holland: technical reference

Dwell Time & Compression Force Optimization

Definition: Dwell time is the interval near maximum punch penetration during a compression event, with its precise definition depending on the press or measurement method. Compression force is the applied load; optimization balances the force-time profile against tablet quality and tooling limits for a specific product.

Three contributing mechanisms:

  1. Production speed or head geometry provides an unsuitable time profile for a strain-rate-sensitive formulation.
  2. Excessive force creates unfavorable stress and elastic recovery, while insufficient force can leave inadequate bonding.
  3. Precompression, air release or decompression is poorly matched to the formulation.

Immediate investigation checklist:

  1. Verify the force calibration and dwell definition, including which compression event is being measured.
  2. Compare force-time traces and quality outcomes with an approved reference; investigate speed, precompression and main compression together.
  3. Run a controlled study within approved equipment/tooling limits, measuring strength, friability, disintegration/dissolution and defects rather than optimizing hardness alone.

Page outline extension: Explain why kN is not pressure: nominal MPa = 1000 × force in kN / projected punch area in mm². Multi-tip tooling requires load-sharing interpretation. For a simplified rotary geometry, dwell ms ≈ 60000 × effective head-flat length / (π × pitch-circle diameter × RPM), with lengths in the same unit; use OEM values for the real press.

Technical references: Natoli: preventing capping; Natoli: punch head inspection

PVD Coatings: CrN vs. DLC vs. TiCN

Definition: Physical vapor deposition (PVD) forms a thin coating from material transferred through a vapor phase to a substrate. CrN and TiCN are hard coating families; diamond-like carbon (DLC) is a family of carbon coatings whose deposition can involve PVD or plasma-assisted CVD, so DLC is not synonymous with PVD.

Three contributing mechanisms:

  1. Surface–formulation interactions that still promote adhesion under the product’s humidity and temperature conditions.
  2. Abrasive wear or concentrated contact loading beyond the coating/substrate system’s capability.
  3. Poor surface preparation, adhesion loss or incompatible cleaning damaging the coating system.

Immediate investigation checklist:

  1. Identify the defect, formulation, substrate, surface finish, cleaning history and existing coating before choosing a candidate.
  2. Obtain coating-specific deposition, thickness, adhesion, temperature and cleaning-compatibility documentation from the supplier.
  3. Compare candidate coatings in a controlled product-specific trial, recording sticking, ejection, wear and quality outcomes.

Page outline extension: Use the comparison below; do not turn generic dry friction data into a pharmaceutical anti-sticking guarantee. Link to existing coating pages rather than duplicating commercial pages.

Technical references: Oerlikon: CrN, TiCN and DLC coating families; Oerlikon: DLC deposition methods; I Holland: formulation/surface adhesion study

2. Functional diagnostic wizard

Open the complete self-contained HTML/CSS/JavaScript widget. The HTML file is also its source code: no framework, account, network request or external library is required.

Logic: first validate user-supplied product reference windows. For each variable classify the observation as below, within or above that window. Combine applicable defect-specific investigation rules and show all matching hypotheses. No probability, diagnosis, universal safe limit or automatic press setting is calculated. A value inside a window does not rule out the defect. Demo limits are explicitly fictional interface examples.

Required context beyond sliders: moisture method and sampling point, formulation, tablet geometry, press/tool configuration, force calibration, precompression and ejection data, tooling history, and batch quality results. Moisture % from different methods is not interchangeable. Slider domains are interface ranges, not qualified operating limits; adapt them only after defining the intended application.

Production acceptance: technical reviewer approval of rules and wording; test against documented cases including conflicting causes; keyboard and mobile checks; no-JavaScript guidance; local-only inputs; no batch data in analytics. Public methodology should state that the app supports investigation. Retain clear non-prescriptive limitations adjacent to results.

4. Five research studies to develop

Research proposals only. No data have been collected for these studies and no findings are claimed.

  1. Friction under controlled conditions: Compare matched coating/substrate coupons using defined counterface, load, speed, roughness, humidity and temperature. Report coefficient-of-friction distributions, replicates and uncertainty. Do not equate dry sliding friction with powder adhesion or tablet sticking.
  2. Coating life and cleaning exposure: Compare cycles to a predefined damage endpoint under documented cleaning regimens. Record product, load, coating and substrate; use survival analysis when tools remain in service at study end.
  3. Tooling-related downtime: Compare matched line-level downtime per scheduled production hour before and after an inspection program. Separate planned and unplanned downtime; account for production mix and report variation. Association alone is not causality.
  4. Contributors to sticking: Audit documented investigations using predetermined evidence and adjudication rules. Allow multiple contributing factors, disclose unresolved cases and give denominators. A convenience sample is not an industry-wide incidence estimate.
  5. Product-specific compression profiles: Compare authorized paired runs within qualified limits, recording force-time profiles, capping rejects, tablet sample counts and other quality attributes. Analyze run/product-level clustering and do not generalize a successful setting to other formulations.

Dataset structure: study ID, anonymous site ID, product family, run ID, tool ID, press/tool geometry, substrate, coating specification, cleaning history, moisture method/value, main and precompression values, dwell definition/value, cycles, downtime categories, defect counts, tablet sample counts, observation dates, instrument calibration, evidence and reviewer. Use separate tables for tools, runs, inspection events and laboratory friction tests so unrelated measurements are not collapsed into one row.

Report outline: research question → cohort and dates → sampling/recruitment → inclusion/exclusion and missing data → methods and definitions → results with denominators and uncertainty → limitations and conflicts → deidentified data/code where permission allows → expert review → version and corrections policy. Obtain permission for customer data and images; remove confidential formulation and site identifiers.
