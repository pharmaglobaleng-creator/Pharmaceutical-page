Interface engineering

Choosing a Surface Strategy for Pharmaceutical Tooling

Polishing, microfinishing, repair, and coatings change different aspects of a tool. The correct choice begins with the failure mechanism and the surfaces that perform the work.

Do not begin with a favorite coating

A surface treatment is not a universal cure for sticking, wear, corrosion, or release difficulty. Similar symptoms can arise from different mechanisms, and the same treatment may behave differently across tool steels, preparations, coating architectures, formulations, cleaning practices, and press conditions.

Start by defining the contact: punch face against formulation, die wall during compaction and ejection, head and cam interface, barrel and guide, or an engraved feature where local geometry matters. Then define the dominant concern—adhesion, abrasion, galling, corrosion, friction, edge integrity, cleanability, or dimensional recovery.

Four different interventions

Controlled polishing

May address localized surface damage or adhered material when the approved method preserves critical geometry and does not mask the underlying cause.

Microfinish engineering

Focuses on a defined surface condition and process. A roughness number alone may not describe directionality, waviness, defects, or functional texture.

Restoration

Attempts to recover function within established limits. It requires pre- and post-work evidence and is inappropriate where integrity or geometry cannot be assured.

Coating

Adds an engineered surface system whose selection must consider substrate, preparation, adhesion, thickness, edge effects, operating contact, and cleaning exposure.

Questions that narrow the selection

  • Where exactly does the symptom begin, and does it repeat by station, feature, time, or product?
  • What is the substrate and its verified condition before treatment?
  • Which dimensions, engravings, edges, or interfaces can tolerate material removal or added thickness?
  • What cleaning agents, temperatures, contact loads, and maintenance methods will the surface encounter?
  • How will performance be evaluated after the change, and what outcome would disprove the hypothesis?
Technical limitation: coating names such as TiN, TiCN, CrN, and DLC identify broad material families, not a complete performance specification. Deposition process, architecture, thickness, preparation, substrate, and application conditions can materially affect results.

Connect treatment to evidence
