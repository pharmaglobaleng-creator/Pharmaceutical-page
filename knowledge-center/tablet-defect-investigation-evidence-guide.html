Root-cause practice

Investigating Tablet Compression Defects Without Guessing

Sticking, picking, capping, lamination, chipping, binding, and weight variation are observations. A disciplined investigation still has to establish the mechanism and contributing conditions.

Describe the event before naming the cause

A strong problem statement records what happened, where, when, and under which conditions. “Picking on the embossed region of the upper punch after two hours” is more useful than “bad tooling.” Include the affected press, station pattern, product, lot, tooling set, speed, force profile, environmental context, and the point in the run when the event appeared.

Keep observations separate from hypotheses. Material adhered to a punch face is an observation. Moisture, surface texture, formulation behavior, temperature, engraving geometry, and cleaning residue are possible contributors that require evidence.

A practical investigation sequence

Contain and preserveFollow the site quality system, retain representative tablets and tooling evidence, and avoid cleaning away the only useful surface information before it is documented.
Map the patternDetermine whether the issue follows a station, punch position, product feature, time interval, material lot, press condition, or operator intervention.
Build competing hypothesesConsider tooling, formulation, environment, equipment, setup, cleaning, lubrication, and measurement—not one favored cause.
Test discriminating evidenceChoose checks that separate the hypotheses. A result should change the ranking of possible causes.
Verify the responseDocument the action, assess its effect under controlled conditions, and monitor recurrence rather than declaring success after one favorable observation.

Evidence streams that should meet

Product and process

Tablet attributes, press parameters, temporal trends, in-process results, material history, and environmental data.

Tooling and equipment

Surface condition, dimensions, engraving, wear location, station mapping, setup, feeder behavior, and mechanical observations.

Quality records

Deviations, maintenance, cleaning, changes, prior occurrences, sampling rationale, and approved disposition criteria.

A correlation is not automatically a cause. If a defect occurs with worn tooling, the investigation must still ask whether the wear created the failure, resulted from the same underlying condition, or is incidental.

How the site supports the investigation

The compression-defects guide organizes visible symptoms. Dedicated pages address sticking and picking, capping, lamination, binding, and weight variation. Where the evidence points toward tooling or press behavior, press troubleshooting, inspection, and failure analysis provide different scopes. These pages are starting points for investigation design, not universal corrective-action prescriptions.
