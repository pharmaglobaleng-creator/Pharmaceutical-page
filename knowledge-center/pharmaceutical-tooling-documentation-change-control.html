Traceable decisions

Documentation That Makes Tooling History Usable

A tooling record should let another qualified person understand what the tool is, what happened to it, what evidence supports its status, and which approved decision followed.

Records connect physical tooling to the quality system

Tablet tooling moves through receipt, storage, setup, production, cleaning, inspection, maintenance, external service, and retirement. If identity or history is lost between those steps, later investigators may be unable to connect a defect to a station, set, intervention, or change.

Documentation should be proportionate to risk and integrated with the manufacturer’s approved procedures. A service report is supporting evidence; it does not replace the manufacturer’s batch records, equipment logs, deviation system, change control, or quality-unit responsibilities.

A practical record architecture

Identity

Set, tool, drawing, revision, material, coating, engraving, quantity, supplier, receipt, and status identifiers.

Event history

Use, cleaning, inspection, maintenance, restoration, damage, deviation, movement, and retirement records with dates and responsible functions.

Decision evidence

Measurements, images, acceptance criteria, exceptions, approvals, limitations, and links to the controlled record that authorized disposition.

Change control asks more than “what changed?”

A proposed tooling change may affect dimensions, engraving, surface condition, coating, material, maintenance method, cleaning, press setup, tablet appearance, or process interaction. The assessment should define the rationale, affected products and documents, technical and quality risks, required qualification or validation work, implementation controls, and post-change monitoring.

ICH Q10 describes change management and process-performance monitoring as elements of a pharmaceutical quality system across the product lifecycle. ICH Q9(R1) provides a framework for risk assessment, control, communication, and review. Neither framework makes every change identical; they support decisions with a level of formality appropriate to uncertainty, importance, and complexity.

Avoid false precision. Do not add invented tolerances, review signatures, inspection results, or compliance statements to make a report appear complete. An explicit evidence gap is safer and more useful than a fabricated answer.

What an external engineering package can contribute

  • A received-item inventory and traceable identification scheme.
  • A defined scope, methods, results, photographs, and noted limitations.
  • Comparison with customer-supplied drawings or acceptance criteria.
  • A clear description of maintenance, restoration, or surface work performed.
  • Exceptions requiring customer technical or quality review.
  • Files that can be referenced by the manufacturer’s controlled systems.
