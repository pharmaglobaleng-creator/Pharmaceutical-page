What does the dipping nozzle funnel cone do?

The dipping nozzle funnel cone helps guide counted tablets or capsules from the counting section toward the bottle or receiving container. Its tapered shape concentrates the discharge path so product enters the container in a controlled, centered stream instead of spreading across a wider area.

This function becomes especially important at production speed. A correctly fitted cone helps reduce product scatter, bouncing and missed container openings while supporting a clean transfer from the counting head to the package below.

Why the condition of this part matters

The funnel cone is a relatively simple-looking component, but its geometry and surface condition can influence how tablets or capsules leave the counter. Cracks, deformation, rough edges, worn surfaces or an incorrect opening size can interfere with the discharge path. Product may hesitate at the transition, contact damaged surfaces, bounce at the container opening or accumulate where the flow should remain smooth.

For products that chip easily or have friable coatings, unnecessary impact and rough contact surfaces can contribute to cosmetic damage or broken product. For high-speed counting applications, even a small alignment problem may become more noticeable as line speed increases. This is why replacement should be based on the installed geometry and machine configuration rather than appearance alone.

During inspection, look for wear around the outlet, distortion of the cone profile, damaged mounting features, product buildup and any signs that the cone no longer centers correctly over the bottle path. The surrounding dipping-nozzle assembly and its mating components should also be checked because alignment problems can originate outside the cone itself.

Fit and replacement checks

  • Confirm the exact Cremer machine configuration and serial information.
  • Compare the installed cone profile, inlet and outlet diameters and overall height.
  • Verify mounting features and how the cone interfaces with the dipping-nozzle assembly.
  • Check product-contact material and required surface finish.
  • Confirm clearance and alignment with the bottle or container opening.
  • Review the existing component or drawing before manufacture whenever possible.

When replacement may be appropriate

Replacement may be considered when the funnel cone is cracked, visibly worn, distorted, difficult to clean, no longer aligns properly or causes repeated product-transfer problems that cannot be corrected through setup. A replacement should reproduce the required functional geometry and material characteristics of the installed component.

Frequently asked questions

Is this part used to count the tablets?

No. The cone does not perform the electronic or mechanical counting function. It works downstream of the counting section by helping guide the already-counted product into the receiving container.

Can the wrong funnel cone affect filling performance?

Yes. An incorrect size, profile or alignment can change the discharge path and may contribute to product scatter, bouncing, hang-up or poor alignment with the container opening.
